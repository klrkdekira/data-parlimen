## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Patents Act 1983, which is referred to as the "Act" in this Statement.

2. Clause 2 of the Bill seeks to amend section 3 of the Act to introduce several new definitions. The definitions of "filing date", "owner of the patent", "prescribed", "priority date", "process" and "product" are introduced to remove ambiguities.

3. Clause 3 of the Bill seeks to amend subsection 4(2) of the Act. At the moment all members of the Patents Board, including those representing bodies and organisations specifically mentioned in that section, are appointed by the Minister. With the proposed amendment those bodies and organisations will be able to appoint their own representatives. Clause 3 also seeks to amend subsection 4(3) of the Act. At the moment all members of the Patents Board, except the representative of the Ministry of Trade and Industry and the representative of the Ministry of Science, Technology and the Environment, hold office for a maximum period of two years. They are, however, eligible for reappointment. With the amendment, the tenure of office of every member whose appointment is made by the body or organisation he represents is no longer subject to the maximum period of two years.

4. Clause 6 of the Bill seeks to amend subsection 14(2) of the Act to redefine "prior art". The amendment makes anything relating

<!-- page 25 -->

to an invention which is disclosed anywhere in the world prior to the priority date of a patent application claiming that invention part of the prior art and this will affect the patentability of the invention. At the moment disclosures made orally, for example, would only constitute "prior art" if they are made in Malaysia. Clause 6 also seeks to make it clear that if the use of any substance or composition in a method for the treatment of the human or animal body or in diagnostic methods practised on the human or animal body is not part of the prior art, then the fact that the substance or composition itself is part of the prior art does not exclude the patentability of such use.

5. Clause 7 of the Bill seeks to replace the existing definition of "utility innovation" with a new definition which is wider. This will enable more innovations to be protected under the Act, thus encouraging the development of technology in the country. Inventions have also been included in the proposed new definition as some inventors may prefer to apply for a certificate for a utility innovation rather than a patent.

6. Clause 9 of the Bill seeks to introduce a new section 17B which allows the conversion of an application for a patent into an application for a certificate for a utility innovation, and vice versa. Clause 9 also seeks to introduce a new section 17C which prohibits the grant of both a patent and a certificate for a utility innovation for the same invention. This will ensure that an inventor does not enjoy overwhelming monopoly in respect of his invention.

7. Clause 10 of the Bill seeks to amend section 18 of the Act by introducing a new subsection (4) which identifies the person to whom the right to a patent for an invention, made separately and independently by two or more persons, belongs.

8. Clause 12 of the Bill seeks to amend section 25 of the Act to ensure that withdrawals of applications are made in writing. The amendment also seeks to prohibit the revocation of any withdrawal. This prohibition will avoid complications in search procedures.

9. Clause 14 of the Bill seeks to amend section 27 of the Act to ensure that any priority claim made is limited to applications filed during the period of twelve months immediately preceding the filing date of the application in which such claim is made.

10. Clause 5 of the Bill seeks to introduce a new section 27A to make it clear what the priority date of an application is.

11. Clauses 16, 17 and 18 of the Bill seek to amend sections 29 and 30 of the Act and also to introduce a new section 29A. The proposed amendments seek to provide for a simpler preliminary

<!-- page 26 -->

examination and to allow an applicant to apply for a modified substantive examination if a patent or other title of protection has been granted in a prescribed country outside Malaysia in respect of the invention for which a patent is applied. The proposed amendments will expedite the process of examining applications for patents.

12. Clause 19 of the Bill seeks to amend section 31 of the Act. Under the proposed amendment, if two or more persons have separately and independently made an invention and each of them has applied for a patent and the applications have the same priority date, then a patent may be granted on each of such applications.

13. Clause 20 of the Bill seeks to amend section 32 of the Act. At the moment, section 32 specifies the particulars to be recorded in the Register. With the proposed amendment, such details will be prescribed by regulations made under the Act. Changes in such details, should such changes become necessary, will therefore be easier to make.

The proposed amendment also seeks to provide for the making of regulations to prescribe the form in which and the medium on which the Register is to be kept. This will enable the Register to be kept on electronic medium or any other medium.

14. Clause 21 of the Bill seeks to introduce a new section 32A into the Act to exclude notices of any trust from the Register in view of the fact that such notices have no significance in the granting of patents.

15. Clause 22 of the Bill seeks to introduce a new section 33A into the Act to make the Register prima facie evidence of all matters required or authorized by the Act to be entered therein and to make copies or extracts from, inter alia, the Register admissible as evidence in court without further proof or production of the original, on condition that such copies or extracts are certified by the Registrar in writing under his hand.

Clause 22 of the Bill also seeks to introduce two new sections, namely, sections 33B and 33c, into the Act. The proposed new section 33B will allow amendments to be made by the Registrar to the Register, when requested to do so by the owner of a patent, for the purpose of correcting errors or entering changes in the name or address of such owner. The proposed new section 33c will enable the Court, upon the application of any aggrieved person, to order the rectification of the Register.

16. Clause 23 of the Bill seeks to amend section 35 of the Act to make it clear that if annual fees are not paid in respect of a patent, the patent shall lapse. The proposed amendment also seeks to require the publication in the Gazette of a notice of the lapsing of the patent.

<!-- page 27 -->

17. Clause 24 of the Bill seeks to introduce a new section 35A into the Act to empower the Registrar to reinstate a lapsed patent subject to certain conditions.

18. Clause 33 of the Bill seeks to amend section 56 of the Act to introduce a new ground for the invalidation of a patent. Under the proposed new paragraph (2)(e), if a person or his agent deliberately provides or causes to be provided incomplete or incorrect information to the Registrar under the new subsection 29A{4) and a patent is subsequently granted to such person, any aggrieved person may apply to the Court for the invalidation of the patent.

19. Clause 38 of the Bill seeks to amend section 79 of the Act primarily to provide for the payment of fees as prescribed by regulations instead of the payment of costs as the Registrar thinks just in respect of requests for amendments to correct clerical errors or obvious mistakes on patent applications and other documents submitted at the Patent Registration Office.

20. Clause 39 of the Bill seeks to introduce a new section 79A which allows amendments, after a patent is granted, to the specifications of a patent or to any other document associated with a patent for the purpose of correcting a clerical error or an obvious mistake. This amendment is an extension of the provisions of section 79 of the Act.

21. Clause 42 of the Bill seeks to introduce a new section 83A into the Act to enable the Registrar to certify that any entry, matter or thing required to be made or done under the Act has been made or done. It also seeks to provide for the admissibility of such certificates in all courts. This will facilitate proceedings in courts on matters relating to the Act.

22. Clause 44 of the Bill seeks to amend subsection 86(5) of the Act to remove the requirement that persons who are not domiciled or resident in Malaysia must institute or appear in suits in respect of their patents through patent agents.

23. Clause 47 of the Bill seeks to amend section 90 of the Act to insert express references to the grant of a patent in Sarawak and to validate grants made in the transitional period under subsection 90(1) or 90(2) of the Act. Such references were unintentionaly omitted and grants may have been made in the transitional period.

24. Clause 49 of the Bill deals with transitional and saving provisions. This clause makes it clear that amendments relating to preliminary and substantive examinations of patent applications will not apply to requests for such examinations, or to such examinations, which are still pending immediately before the amendments came into force. The existing provisions will continue to apply to such requests and examinations.

<!-- page 28 -->

25. Apart from the provisions which have been specifically dealt with in the preceding paragraphs of this Statement, several provisions have been included in the Bill for the purpose of correcting errors in the national language text of the Act. Such provisions are contained in clauses 14, 25, 28, 29, 30, 32, 35, 36, 37 and 43 of the Bill.

26. Other amendments proposed in this Bill but not specifically dealt with in this Statement are minor or consequential in nature.
