## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to introduce some amendments to the Courts of Judicature Act 1964 ("the Act").

2. Clause 2 seeks to amend section 50 of the Act to include in the jurisdiction of the Court of Appeal the power to hear and determine any appeal against any decision made by the High Court in the exercise of its revisionary jurisdiction. This clause also seeks to provide that any appeal on a question of law to the Court of Appeal from the decision of a Magistrate's Court must be a question of law which has arisen in the course of the appeal or revision and that the determination of such question by the High Court had affected the event of the appeal or revision. This clause further seeks to impose a time limit for obtaining leave from the Court of Appeal. Clause 3 seeks to amplify the operation of this time limit.

3. Clause 4 seeks to delete the proviso to subsection 52(1) of the Act.

4. Clauses 5 and 6 seek to amend subsections 53(2) and 57(1) of the Act to substitute the words "trial Court" with the words "High Court" in order to make it clear that the Court concerned is the High Court and not any court subordinate thereto.

5. Clause 7 inter alia seeks to amend subsection 60(2) of the Act for the same reason given for clauses 5 and 6. It is consequential upon the proposed amendment to section 50 of the Act.

6. Clause 8 seeks to amend subsection 61(3) of the Act consequential upon the abolition of trials by jury.

7. Clause 9 seeks to amend section 63 of the Act consequential upon the proposed amendments to section 50 of the Act.

8. Clause 10 seeks to delete section 65 of the Act.

9. Clause 11 seeks to delete section 66 of the Act consequential upon the proposed amendments to subsection 50(2) of the Act.

<!-- page 6 -->

10. Clause 12 seeks to make consequential amendments to subsection 67(1) of the Act.

11. Clauses 13 and 15 seek to amend sections 92 and 95 respectively of the Act in order to remove possible ambiguity in the reference to "trial court" arising from the introduction of the Court of Appeal. It is also consequential to the proposed amendment to section 50 of the Act.

12. Clause 14 seeks to amend section 93 of the Act in order also to remove possible ambiguity in the reference to "trial court" and also to delete reference to jury and assessors consequential upon the abolition of trials by jury.

13. Clause 16 seeks to amend section 96 of the Act to enable appeals on certain matters to be made without having to have regard for the monetary value of the claim concerned. This is necessary because the particularisation of the existing paragraph (a) of section 96 of the Act no longer allows appeals from decisions in certain civil matters, in particular matters concerning judicial review of administrative actions which ordinarily cannot be given monetary value, to be brought before the Federal Court.

14. Clause 17 provides for saving and transitional provisions to enable proceedings pending before the Supreme Court on the 23rd June 1994 to be proceeded before the Pederal Court.
