## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Town and Country Planning Act 1976 ("the Act").

2. Clause I seeks to deal with the short title and commencement of this Bill.

3. Clause 2 seeks to define certain new words and expressions used in this Bill.

4. Clause 3 seeks to amend subsection 4(1) of the Act to introduce four new members to the State Planning Committee.

5. Clause 4 seeks to amend subsection 10(5) of the Act to extend the period for the State Planning Committee to approve a draft structure plan from three months to six months.

6. Clause 5 seeks to amend paragraph 12(3)(a) of the Act to provide for additional matters to be incorporated in the draft local plan.

7. Clause 6 seeks to introduce a new subsection (3) to section 13 to enable the extension of the period for making objections to or representations in respect of the draft local plan.

8. Clause 8 seeks to introduce three new sections. The new section 2lA deals with the requirement on the submission of a development proposal report when applying for planning permission and its particulars. The new section 21B gives details of the layout plans. Amongst others, the layout plans shall contain measures for the protection of the physical environment, preservation of landscapes, natural topography and trees, making up of open spaces and the preservation and enhancement of the character and appearance of buildings with special architecture or historical interest. The new section 21c requires a qualified person to prepare all documents, drawings, layout plans, maps, models, plans, particulars and reports required to be submitted under the Act.

9. Clause 9 seeks to amend section 22 of the Act to ensure that the local planning authority shall have regard to the development proposal report when considering an application for planning permission, and in granting planning permission the local planning authority shall have the power to impose certain new conditions.

10. Clauses 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14 seek to increase the penalties for offences under sections 26, 27, 28, 29 and 30 of the Act respectively. Penalty of fifty thousand ringgit is increased to one hundred thousand ringgit. Penalty of ten and twenty thousand ringgit

<!-- page 17 -->

is increased to fifty thousand ringgit. Penalty for a continuous offence is increased from five hundred ringgit to one thousand ringgit for every day that the offence is continued after conviction.

11. Clause 15 seeks to introduce into the Act, a new Part VA which contains the following provisions:

The new section 35A seeks to deal with the tree preservation order.

Under subsection (1) the local planning authority is empowered to make a tree preservation order. Subsection (2) sets out the provisions that may be made in a tree preservation order.

Subsection (3) deals with situations where permission to fell, cut down, top, lop or uproot a tree is not required.

Subsection (4) provides a penalty for a contravention of this section. Subsection (5) deals with the presumption of liability on the occupier of the land.

The new section 35B seeks to exempt the imposition of a tree preservation order in respect of trees already subjected to conditions on preservation of trees under paragraphs (f) and (g) of subsection 22 (5).

The new section 35c seeks to give the right of appeal to a person aggrieved by a tree preservation order and matters related thereto.

The new section 35D seeks to provide for compensation to be paid in consequence of a tree preservation order. Subsection (1) seeks to enable the owner of the land to claim compensation if he proves that he has suffered damages in the form of depreciation in the value of the land in consequence of the tree preservation order.

Subsection (2) deals with the time for filing such claim. If he is dissatisfied with the amount of compensation awarded, he may appeal to the Appeal Board under subsection (3).

The new section 35E pertains to replacement of trees.

Subsection (1) imposes a duty on the person found guilty under subsection 35A (4) for felling, cutting down, topping, lopping, uprooting, damaging or destroying a tree in contravention of a tree preservation order to replace such tree.

Such tree shall be replaced within the time specified by the local planning authority which may be extended once under subsection

(2) on application.

<!-- page 18 -->

Subsection (3) provides for appeal.

Subsection (4) requires the application of the tree preservation order to a tree replanted under subsection (1) as it had applied to the original tree.

Subsection (5) contains the penalty for non-compliance of this

Where the person whose duty it is to replace a tree fails to do so, the local planning authority may proceed to replace the tree under the new section 35F and all costs and expenses incurred thereby by it shall be reimbursed by him.

The new section 35G seeks to confer powers on the local planning authority to amend or revoke a tree preservation order.

The new section 35H seeks to prohibit the felling, cutting down, topping, lopping or uprooting of a tree with a girth exceeding 0.8 metre except in respect and for the purposes of the circumstances in paragraphs (a), (b) and (c) of subsection (1). Subsection (2) deals with the manner for measuring the girth of a tree. Subsection (3) contains the penalty for an offence under this section.

12. Clause 16 seeks to amend subsection 36(10) of the Act to regularize the procedure and powers of the Appeal Board.

13. Clause 17 seeks to introduce a new section 52A to provide that where offences are committed by a body corporate, its directors or officers shall be deemed to be guilty of those offences as well as the body corporate itself.

14. Clause 18 seeks to extend the power of the State Authority to makes rules arising out of the matters introduced in the other proposed amendments in this Bill. The new subsection (3) to section 58 of this Bill seeks to ensure that the entitlement of a person to submit plans, documents, particulars and layout plans relating to town and country planning under any other written law is not affected although his qualification may not be prescribed under paragraph 58(2)(h) of the Act.

15. Other amendments in this Bill which have not been specifically referred to are amendments which are minor or consequential in nature.
