## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Customs Act 1967 ("the Act").

2. Clause 2 seeks to amend the definition of "inland clearance depot" by deleting the words "in transit" so as not to restrict activities in the depot to the handling and temporary storage of goods in transit but also to allow the clearance of goods at the depot.

3. Clause 3 seeks to amend subsection 3(3) of the Act to substitute the word "section" with the words "sections 13B,". This is a consequential amendment necessitated by the introduction of the new section 13B.

4. Clause 4 seeks to introduce a new section 8A to empower the Minister to prescribe uniforms, rank markings, authority cards and badges for officers of customs.

<!-- page 5 -->

5. Clause 5(a) seeks to amend section 9 of the Act to require an officer of customs to produce on demand, in addition to the authority card, such badge as the Director General may direct to be carried by such officer. Clause 5(b) seeks to provide for the surrender of the badge and authority card of an officer of customs when he ceases to be such officer of customs or when directed to do so.

6. Clause 6 seeks to introduce a new section 9A to make it an offence for a person to unlawfully wear, use, possess or display a prescribed uniform, badge or authority card, otherwise than in a theatrical performance.

7. Clause 7 seeks to delete subsection 11(6). This provision is re- enacted as a new section 22B vide clause 10.

8. In the process of ascertaining the customs duty leviable on goods, the proper officer of customs has, inter alia, to determine the class of goods to which such dutiable or uncustomed goods belong. Clause 8 seeks to amend subsection 13(1) to enable the proper officer of customs not only to value goods but also to classify them.

9. Clause 9 seeks to introduce two new provisions. The proposed section 13A seeks to enable a person who is dissatisfied with a classification or valuation to pay the customs duty under protest. The proposed section 13B seeks to enable the Director General to make a decision on any questions on classification that are referred to him.

10. Clause 10 seeks to introduce two new sections. The proposed section 22A seeks to provide that where the Director General has made a decision under section 13B or 22, the production of a certificate signed by the Director General in respect of such decision shall be admissible in evidence and shall be sufficient evidence of the facts stated therein without the need to call the Director General to give evidence in court.

11. Clause 11 seeks to amend section 129 to enable the Director General to order payment to be made before goods or amounts forfeited are delivered or refunded to the owner or person entitled thereto.

12. Clause 12 seeks to amend section 131 of the Act to increase the amount of compound that may be accepted in respect of cases under paragraphs (a), (b), (c), (d) and (e) of subsection 135(1). The amount of compound that may be accepted shall be a sum not less than two times and not more than ten times the customs duty.

<!-- page 6 -->

13. Clause 13 seeks to amend section 138 of the Act to provide that any breach of the conditions and restriction of an exemption granted constitutes an offence. The penalty has also been increased from a fine not exceeding five thousand ringgit to a fine not exceeding ten thousand ringgit.
