## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to make comprehensive provisions for the licensing and regulation of insurers, insurance brokers and adjusters. Upon becoming law, it will replace the existing Insurance Act 1963 (repealed Act). The provisions in this Bill are based, with certain modifications and additions, on the existing provisions in the repealed Act.

2. Part I of this Bill deals with preliminary matters, such as the definitions of certain terms used in the Bill and empowering the Bank with all the functions conferred on it by the Bill, including authorising an employee or person to perform any of its functions.

<!-- page 137 -->

3. Part II provides for the licensing of persons carrying on insurance business, insurance broking business or adjusting business.

Clause 4 seeks to classify insurance business into general business and life business.

Clause 5 provides that employees retirement scheme, business carried on by mutual benefit society under the Societies Act 1966 and both export credit and other credit insurance business carried on by Malaysia Export Credit Insurance Berhad (MECIB) are not insurance business. MECIB, will therefore operate outside the purview of the Bill.

Clause 6 empowers the Bank to prohibit or restrict a licensed insurer from carrying on specific description of insurance business in order to prevent imprudent underwriting.

Clause 7 prohibits a licensed insurer from carrying on annuity certain or investment-linked insurance business except with the approval of the Bank because these types of insurance have to be regulated differently.

Clause 8 sets out the activities which constitute insurance business.

Clauses 9 and 10 prohibit a person from carrying on licensed business i.e. insurance business, insurance broking business or adjusting business or holding out as an insurer, insurance broker or adjuster, unless licensed, respectively. Subclause 9(2) exempts aviation or maritime loss adjusters from being licensed under this Bill.

Clause 11 prohibits the use of the words "insurance", "assurance" or "underwriter" or any other words capable of being construed as indicating the carrying on of any of the licensed businesses without the Bank's approval. This prohibition does not apply to a licensee and a person exempted under subclause 11(2).

Clause 12 prohibits a licensed insurer from carrying on both life business and general business. This prohibition does not apply to a licensed professional reinsurer or a licensed insurer which is already lawfully carrying on both businesses. Since life and general business are substantially different, it is considered desirable to separate the two activities. However, life insurers will be permitted to transact health insurance business.

Clause 13 provides for the Minister to be the licensing authority for insurers and for the Bank to be the licensing authority for insurance brokers and adjusters.

<!-- page 138 -->

Clause 14 provides for an applicant for a licence for insurance business to be a public company, for insurance broking business or adjusting business to be a company and for professional reinsurance business to be a body corporate.

Clause 15 provides for the mode of application for a licence under this Bill.

Clauses 16 and 17 provide for the licensing of insurers, insurance brokers and adjusters, other than aviation and maritime adjusters.

Subclauses 18(1) and (2) provide for a local insurer's minimum paid-up share capital, or a foreign insurer's surplus of assets over liabilities, to be such amount as may be prescribed by the Bank. This is to ensure that licensed insurers have the desired minimum financial strength.

Subclause 18(3) provides for the minimum paid-up share capital of an insurance broker and adjuster to be prescribed by the Bank.

Clauses 19 and 20 set out the requirements as to statement of capital and prospectus.

Clause 21 provides for licence fees for all licensees to be prescribed. A licensed insurer is also required to pay licence fees upon the Bank's approval for its establishment of an office in Malaysia other than its principal place of business and an annual licence fee for its other offices.

Clause 22 provides for mandatory membership of appropriate trade association to encourage self-regulation by the insurance industry.

Clause 23 enables the imposition of conditions on a licence.

Clauses 24, 25 and 26 deal with the form and display of the licence and the affixing of a licensee's name outside its office.

Clause 27 requires a licensed insurer to commence business within twelve months from the date of its licence.

Clause 28 restricts licensees from carrying on unrelated activities.

Clause 29 requires a licensee to obtain the written approval of the Bank before amending its licensing particulars, including its constituent documents.

Clause 30 requires a licensee to exclusively manage its licensed business unless approved by the Bank but a licensed insurer is allowed to operate jointly with other licensed insurers or to co- insure with one or more licensed insurers.

<!-- page 139 -->

Clause 31 lists the circumstances in which a licence may be revoked.

Clause 32 provides for appeal against a revocation of licence.

Clause 33 sets out the responsibilities of a licensee whose licence is revoked and specifically makes it subject to continued application of relevant provisions of the Bill in order to protect policy owner's interests during the period of winding down of the business.

Clause 34 requires the Bank to publish a list of licensed insurers not later than 31 March each year.

4. Part III deals with subsidiary and office of licensee.

Clauses 35, 36 and 37 prohibit a licensee from establishing any subsidiary or office in or outside Malaysia without the approval of the Bank and provide for the application for such approval.

5. Part IV deals with insurance funds set up by licensed insurers and matters related to insurance funds and shareholders' funds.

Clauses 38 and 39 require a licensed insurer to establish separate insurance funds for its Malaysian and for its foreign policies for life and general business and the licensed insurer has to allocate its policies to the respective insurance funds.

Clause 40 requires incomes to be credited to the respective insurance funds.

Clause 41 requires that an insurance fund should at all times be adequate to meet its liabilities and that assets of an insurance fund are to be applied only to meet its liabilities and expenses.

Clause 42 requires the assets of an insurance fund to be separate from the insurer's other assets and excludes certain intangible assets from being included as assets of an insurance fund.

Clause 43 regulates the manner and quantum of withdrawal from an insurance fund.

Clause 44 empowers the Bank to prescribe the manner of valuing assets and determining liabilities. This will ensure that the balance sheet presents the true financial condition of the licensed insurer.

Clause 45 regulates the manner of investment of assets of a licensed insurer.

<!-- page 140 -->

Clause 46 provides for the solvency margin to be prescribed and requires a licensed insurer to hold assets representing its margin of solvency within its insurance fund unless approved otherwise by the Bank. This is an important safety cushion for policy owners.

Clause 47 requires a licensed insurer to maintain a register of policies and a register of insurance claims. This will make it an offence to delay or omit to recognise liabilities of the licensed insurer.

Clause 48 requires all assets of a licensed insurer and its document of title or right to an asset to be held in its corporate name or subject to the Bank's approval, in the name of its nominee.

Clauses 49, 50, 51, 52, 53 and 54 require a licensed insurer and a licensed insurance broker not to grant an unsecured credit facility, define security for credit facility, provide for the valuation of security, empower the Bank to prescribe restriction or prohibition of certain properties as security, restrict the pledging of assets of a licensed insurer and a licensed insurance broker and require the disclosure of interest in credit facility by director, chief executive officer or credit manager of a licensed insurer and a licensed insurance broker, respectively.

Clause 55 requires a director of a licensed insurer and a licensed insurance broker to disclose the fact, nature and extent of any conflict of interest to its board of directors.

Clause 56 requires the secretary of the licensed insurer or licensed insurance broker to record any declaration made under clause 54 or 55.

Clause 57 requires a licensed insurer to reinsure according to sound insurance principles and empowers the Bank to give directions where necessary. This will enable the Bank to ensure that reinsurance arrangements are genuine and are in the best interests of the policy owners.

6. Part V deals with the direction and control of defaulting insurers by the Minister and the Bank.

Clause 58 sets up an early warning system by requiring a licensed insurer which is just complying with solvency margin but having adverse business results or which is deficient in solvency margin to notify the Bank and submit a plan to improve its financial condition and empowers the Bank to issue directions. If there is no surplus of assets over liabilities, the Bank may petition for its winding up after giving it a hearing.

<!-- page 141 -->

Clause 59 empowers the Bank to take action where a licensed insurer is operating in an imprudent manner or contravenes the law or is deficient in solvency margin or its director or officer acts in a prejudicial manner. Subclause 59(3) empowers the Bank with the approval of the Minister to remove an employee or director or appoint an employee or director or adviser and to terminate any contract entered into by the insurer. Subclause 59(4) empowers the Bank upon order of the Minister to assume control of the licensed insurer or to petition the Court to wind up the licensed insurer. Subclauses 59(5), (6) and (7) deal with opportunity of the licensed insurer to submit a representation in the matter. Clauses 60, 61, 62 and 63 are consequential provisions flowing from clause 59.

Clause 64 provides for the Bank to apply to Court to cancel the share capital of a licensed insurer which is not represented by assets, to issue fresh capital and to allot fresh capital to any person if not taken up by present shareholders of the licensed insurer.

Clause 65 empower the Minister to issue directions to the licensed insurer deficient in solvency margin with regard to its business or to suspend its licence or to apply to Court to stay a particular class of actions with regard to the licensed insurer's business for six months, while giving the licensed insurer the opportunity to make representation.

7. Part VI deals with the management of licensees.

Clause 66 empowers a licensee to call for information on beneficial ownership of its shares or shares of the company holding its shares thus ensuring transparency in the shareholding.

Clause 67 makes it necessary to secure the approval of the Minister (for a licensed insurer) or Bank (for a licensed insurance broker and a licensed adjuster) before acquiring or disposing of shares leading to an aggregate shareholding of more than five per cent in the licensee or in its controller.

Subclause 67(2) prohibits a person who has obtained the approval of the Minister or the Bank, as the case may be, for the acquisition or disposal of any interest in shares of a licensee or its controller from entering into any subsequent agreement or arrangement for the acquisition or disposal of any further interest in shares of the licensee or its controller unless he has obtained the prior written approval of the Minister or the Bank, as the case may be, to enter into the subsequent agreement or arrangement.

Clause 68 empowers the Bank to make a preliminary order in writing or restrict any person contravening clause 67 from dealing with the shares.

<!-- page 142 -->

Clause 69 prohibits cross-holding of interest in shares in licensees except with the approval of Minister on the recommendation of the Bank. The prohibition on cross-holding does not apply to interest in shares in licensed insurers carrying on different classes of insurance business,

Clause 70 requires a licensee and a controller of a licensee to seek and obtain the Bank's approval before appointing a director or chief executive officer and requires that the director, chief executive officer or manager to be appointed must be a "fit and proper" person and also a resident in Malaysia during the period of his appointment, except in the case of a non-executive director representing a foreign shareholder.

Clauses 71, 72 and 73 set out the circumstances under which a person would be disqualified from being appointed as director, chief executive officer, manager, secretary or other employee concerned in the management of a licensee, the effect of such disqualification and the requirement for a licensee to notify the Bank when any person has ceased to be its director or chief executive officer and the reasons for the cessation,

8. Part VII deals with matters relating to the auditor, actuary and accounts of a licensee, where applicable.

Clause 74 requires a licensee to appoint each financial year an auditor who is approved by the Bank.

Clause 75 sets out circumstances under which a person would be disqualified from being appointed as an auditor of a licensee.

Clause 76 imposes restrictions on an audit firm which is to be appointed auditor of a licensee.

Clause 77 requires an auditor to give his consent to act as such before his appointment.

Clause 78 provides that the appointment of an audit firm shall operate as an appointment of the members of that firm.

Clause 79 provides that an auditor of a licensee shall not be deemed to be its employee.

Clauses 80 and 81 require an auditor of a licensee to submit a report on its compliance with the law and on its business affairs to the members of the licensee, empower the Bank to require an auditor to submit additional information, or to enlarge the scope of his audit as specified.

<!-- page 143 -->

Clause 82 requires an auditor to report to the Bank any contravention of the Bill or any other law, any serious irregularity or if the margin of solvency is not satisfactorily maintained according to Clause 46 and protects the auditor from liability for such disclosure.

Clauses 83 and 84 provide for a licensed life insurer to appoint each year an actuary who is approved by the Bank and specifies the qualification of an appointed actuary and the circumstances under which an appointed actuary ceases to be the appointed actuary and the action to be taken on such cessation.

Clause 85 requires an annual actuarial investigation into the financial condition of a licensed life insurer's business and requires the appointed actuary's report and certificate relating to the financial condition of the licensed life insurer's business to be lodged with the Bank.

Clause 86 requires a licensee and its director, controller or employee to furnish information which is not false or misleading and not incomplete to its auditor or appointed actuary.

Clause 87 requires a licensee to submit to the Bank its audited profit and loss account and balance sheet, a certificate by its auditor, a report by its board and in the case of a licensed insurer, also its duly audited revenue accounts. This clause also requires the licensee to submit to the Bank any report by the auditor to its board of directors and report on action taken on it by the board.

Clause 88 provides that in respect of a licensed foreign insurer, documents required to be submitted under clauses 85, 87 and 89 need only relate to its Malaysian policies.

Clause 89 requires a licensed insurer to submit quarterly returns to the Bank relating to its operations to enable close monitoring of its business.

Clause 90 requires a licensee to maintain its accounts in compliance with accounting standards approved by the Bank to ensure that the accounts will present a true picture.

Clause 91 empowers the Bank to require a licensed general insurer to obtain an actuarial valuation of its liability in respect of incurred but not reported claims.
