## PART I

## PRELIMINARY

<!-- marginal: Short title. -->

1. This Act may be cited as the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998.

<!-- marginal: Commence- -->

2. This Act comes into operation on a date to be appointed

<!-- marginal: ment. -->

by the Minister by notification in the Gazette, and the Minister may appoint different dates for different provisions of this Act.

<!-- marginal: Objects. -->

3. (1) The objects of this Act are—

(a) to promote national policy objectives for the communications and multimedia industry;

(b) to establish a licensing and regulatory framework in support of national policy objectives for the communications and multimedia industry;

(c) to establish the powers and functions for the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission; and

<!-- page 16 -->

(d) to establish powers and procedures for the administration of this Act.

(2) The national policy objectives for che communications and multimedia industry are—

(a) to establish Malaysia as a major global centre and hub for communications and multimedia information and content services;

(b) to promote a civil society where information- based services will provide the basis of continuing enhancements to quality of work and life;

(c) to grow and nurture local information resources and cultural representation that facilitate the national identity and global diversity;

(d) to regulate for the long-term benefit of the end user;

(e) to promote a high level of consumer confidence in service delivery from the industry;

(f) to ensure an equitable provision of affordable services over ubiquitous national infrastructure;

(g) to create a robust applications environment for end users;

(h) to facilitate the efficient allocation of resources such as skilled labour, capital, knowledge and national assets;

(i) to promote the development of capabilities and skills within Malaysia's convergence industries; and

(j) to ensure information security and network reliability and integrity.

(3) Nothing in this Act shall be construed as permitting the censorship of the Internet.

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to make new and better provisions to regulate the converging communications and multimedia industries.

## OVERVIEW

2. The scope of the proposed Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 ("the Act") covers communications over the electronic media but not the print media. It also does not affect the general application of existing laws on national security, illegal content, defamation and copyright. Such laws are still applicable to all forms of content, regardless of the medium.

3. The Act does not encroach on the scope of other existing legislation regulating traditional media and content services. It does, however, over-ride some specific provisions relevant to traditional media which would otherwise have limiting or counterproductive impact on the development of the communications and multimedia industry.

4. The Act establishes a framework for regulatory intervention to promote Malaysia's national policy objectives for the communications and multimedia industry. This is an evolving industry in which a range of traditionally stand alone industries are converging.

5. The activities and services regulated under this Act include traditional broadcasting, telecommunications, and online services, including the facilities and networks used in providing such services, as well as the content which is supplied via the facilities and networks.

6. The Act seeks to provide a generic set of regulatory provisions based on generic definitions of market and service activities and

<!-- page 141 -->

services. While the communications and multimedia industry may encompass both networked and non-networked services and activities, the jurisdiction of this Act is restricted to networked services and activities only.

7. The key participants in the industry who are regulated under the Act include the following: — network facilities providers, who are the owners of facilities such as satellite earth stations, broadband fibre optic cables, telecommunications lines and

<!-- marginal: exchanges, -->

radiocommunications transmission equipment, mobile communications base stations, and broadcasting transmission towers and equipment; — network services providers, who provide the basic connectivity and bandwidth to support a variety of applications; — applications service providers, who provide particular functions such as voice services, data services, content- based services, electronic commerce and other transmission services; and — content applications service providers who are a special subset of applications service providers including traditional broadcast services and newer services such as online publishing and information services.

8. The Act is arranged in a modular structure. according to the different types of regulatory interventions required, as follows:

<!-- page 142 -->

9. Part I of the Act sets out the objects of the legislation and provides definitions of the terms used in the Act.

10. Parts II to V of the Act establish the powers of the Minister and a new regulatory Commission and an Appeal Tribunal, and sets out procedures for the exercise of these powers.

11. Part VI of the Act establishes the framework for the economic regulation of the communications and multimedia industry, including the control of market entry through licensing, general safeguards to promote fair and sustainable competition in the industry and specific transitional safeguards. In addition, provisions for access to connectivity services ensure that industry participants at each level of the industry value chain have access to the facilities and services needed to deliver their own services.

12. Part VII of the Act addresses the requirements for technical regulation of the industry, including the assignment of limited resources (spectrum, numbering and electronic addressing) and also provides a framework to ensure the safety, security and integrity of network services and applications services through technical codes and standards.

13. Part VIII of the Act sets out the framework for the protection and promotion of consumer interests.

14. Part IX of the Act provides the framework for social regulation, including the promotion of local content, cultural diversity and adherence to prevailing community standards.

15. Part X of the Act provides for general matters.

16. Part XI of the Act establishes arrangements and procedures for the transition from the current regulatory regime to the new regime established under the Act. The Act repeals the Telecommunications Act 1950 and the Broadcasting Act 1988.

## PART I

17. Part I of the Act seeks to provide for the short title and commencement of the Act. It also sets out the objects of the Act, which are to promote the ten national policy objectives for the communications and multimedia industry and to establish the necessary licensing framework and regulatory powers and procedures.

<!-- page 143 -->

18. Part I further seeks to establish the extra-territorial application of the Act which is necessary to ensure that the regime applies to providers outside Malaysia who provide services to, from, or within, Malaysia (for example, by satellite), and to the activities of licensees in international markets which may have effects in Malaysia. The Minister is empowered to exempt certain persons, places, premises or geographical areas from the provisions of the Act.

19. Part I also defines particular words or terms for the purposes of the Act.

## PARTS II TO V

20. Parts II to V of the Act establish the powers of the Minister and the Commission, based on specific regulatory instruments, and also establish a mechanism for the appeal of certain decisions made pursuant to the Act. The structure of the Ministerial and Commission's powers and instruments is shown in the diagram below:

<!-- page 144 -->

Ministerial powers and procedures:

21. Part II establishes the Minister's powers and sets out the procedures that must be followed by the Minister in issuing particular subordinate instruments ("instruments") and executing such powers. All instruments issued by the Minister must be consistent with the objects and provisions of the Act. Instruments must be made public, and maintained in relevant public registers by the Commission, in order to promote transparency of the regulatory regime. This Act includes procedures for the variation or repeal of any instrument. Instruments which may be issued by the Minister include directions to the Commission, determination of a matter, grant of licence, declaration of licence conditions, and regulations.

Direction to the Commission: The Minister may direct the Commission with respect to the Commission's exercise of its powers and performance of its functions and obligations under the Act.

Determination of a matter: The Minister may determine matters under the Act. A determination may be made entirely at the discretion of the Minister, subject to the provisions of the Act, and does not require consultation with the Commission or any other person.

Grant of licences: The Minister has ultimate discretion with respect to the granting of licences under the Act, but must give due consideration to the relevant recommendation of the Commission.

Declaration of licence conditions: The Minister may declare licence conditions and may vary or revoke licence conditions with respect to licences granted under this Act such as individual licences and class licences. The Minister must consult with affected licensees prior to declaring, varying or revoking a licence condition. Procedures for consultation ate provided in the Act.

Regulations: The Act empowers the Minister to make any regulations as may be necessary to give full effect to the Act.

22. The extensive and robust provisions relating to powers and procedures under the Act are important for the following reasons: — firstly, the regulatory framework places strong reliance on instruments to provide for specific regulatory provisions which are expected, and need, to change over time; — secondly, it is important to establish and enable a framework that is forward-looking and that is not quickly overtaken by the pace of industry change;

<!-- page 145 -->

— thirdly, there needs to be a clear and transparent framework to promote investor confidence in the approaches to policy management and administration and to minimise sources of uncertainty.

Appeal Tribunal:

23. Part III of the Act seeks to establish an independent Appeal Tribunal ("Tribunal") which has the power to review the merits of decisions or directions of the Commission upon appeal by a person affected by such decisions or directions. A determination by the Commission is not appealable to the Tribunal. The rationale for the establishment of the Tribunal are— — to provide an expert review body, which provides the industry with an avenue of merit not available through court proceedings; — to promote transparency and eliminate the pressures on the Minister to review detailed administrative decisions; and — to provide a fast and low cost avenue of appeal.

24. The Act provides that members of the Tribunal may be appointed for a number of years, not exceeding three years, but the Tribunal shall only meet on an ad hoc basis as and when it is required to preside over any appeal lodged.

25. The members of the Tribunal are appointed by the Minister and must include a Chairman (who is a Judge of the High Court) and at least two other members. Clause 22 provides that the quorum for the Tribunal shall be a minimum of three persons or a maximum of five persons (including the Chairman).

26. Part III further sets out the procedures for the resignation or termination of appointment of members. It also establishes certain criteria and qualifications which members of the Tribunal must meet. It also seeks to establish procedures for the Tribunal's decision- making and to empower the Minister to determine the operating procedures for the Tribunal.

27. Part IV of the Act seeks to establish procedures for the grant of licences by the Minister. These are standard, procedures which must be followed in relation to the administration of any licence granted under the various Parts of the Act.

28. The Act provides for two types of licences, namely individual licences and class licences. The licensing provisions under the Act are designed to allow flexibility with respect to licensing structures as the licensing requirements vary over time with the evolution of

<!-- page 146 -->

the communications and multimedia industry. The power to decide which type of licence is to be used for a particular type of activity is exercisable at the discretion of the Minister.

29. Standard licence conditions apply to both individual and class licences. These conditions are set out in the Schedule to the Act. All licences must contain the relevant standard conditions. Special or additional licence conditions only apply in respect of individual licences and such licence conditions are declared by the Minister.

30. The Minister may not grant an individual licence to a person where the activities for which the individual licence is requested are subject to a class licence. This is to ensure that a person may not operate under both an individual licence and a class licence with respect to the same activity.

31. Persons seeking an individual licence under the Act must submit a written application to the Commission. The Commission is required to process the application and provide a recommendation to the Minister as to whether an individual licence should be granted.

32. The Minister must give a written notice of the granting of an individual licence setting out (by declaration) any special or additional conditions to which the individual licence shall be subject. The Minister must also give written notice of a refusal to grant an individual licence including the reasons for the refusal.

, 33. Part IV seeks to empower the Minister to vary or revoke individual licence conditions at any time, provided that the procedures set out in this Part, for notifying the licensee and permitting the licensee to appeal regarding the proposed variation or revocation, are followed.

34. A licensee may surrender his individual licence at any time by written notice to the Minister. The Commission may recommend to the Minister that an individual licence be cancelled in certain circumstances which are specified in this Part. The Minister may cancel an individual licence by declaration but must give the licensee the opportunity to appeal against the proposed cancellation or provide reasons for his alleged conduct or position. An individual licence may not be assigned, sub-licensed or transferred to any other party unless the prior written approval of the Minister has been granted.

35. In order to promote transparency of the regulatory regime, the Minister must provide a copy of all individual licences granted under the Act and related decisions issued under this Part, including individual licence conditions, declarations of variations or revocations of individual licence provisions and notices of surrender or cancellation, to the Commission. 69. Part VI of the Act seeks to establish a framework for the economic regulation of the communications and multimedia industry. The purposes of the economic regulatory interventions are to promote consumer markets which offer choice, quality and affordability, any-to-any connectivity, competition in markets and investment and innovation in the sector. The diagram below outlines the overall structure of Part VI and shows the main areas of responsibility for both the Minister and the Commission:

70. Within the economic regulation module of the Act there are three primary Chapters: licensing of market participants, general competition practices, and regulation of access to services. These are discussed below.

Licensing:

71. Chapter 1 of Part VI establishes the licensing requirements of the industry. The objective of this Chapter is to both control and monitor the entry of network facilities providers and network service providers into the market, and to ensure that all facilities and services are provided in accordance with national policy. In addition, licensing allows the imposition of various conditions on market participation.

72. Subject to such exemptions as may be determined by the Minister, the Act precludes the ownership of network facilities, or the provision of network services, or the provision of applications services except under and in accordance with an individual licence or a class licence granted by the Minister under the Act. The Act does not prescribe a specific licensing structure. Rather it allows the Minister flexibility to grant either individual licences or class licences for particular types of activities as the Minister deems fit. This flexibility is designed to ensure that the Act remains responsible to changing requirements as the industry evolves.

73. Part VI of the Act allows the Minister to declare that a licensed network facilities provider, other than the owner of any network facilities, is the nominated facilities provider for the network facilities for the purposes of the Act if such nomination will not impede the objects or the efficient administration of the Act. This provision will enable infrastructure ordinarily used by its owner for non-public purposes to be used by a nominated network facilities provider to provide services to the public thereby promoting the efficient use of resources. This provision will also allow for financing arrangements which may otherwise require the financier to be licensed under the Act.

74. Part VI specifically exempts any applications service provider who is not subject to a class licence from the prohibitions under the Act. This Part confirms that some applications service providers may not be subject to individual licences and also envisages that some applications service providers will not require a licence under the Act. The purpose of the provisions in this Part is to exclude small applications service providers or persons who may inadvertently fall within the definition of an applications service provider from the licensing requirements under the Act.

75. Part VI of the Act provides for three categories of competition regulation, which apply to a communications market. A communications market has been defined to be an economic market for—

— network services; — applications services; — goods and services used in conjunction with the above; or — access to network facilities used in conjunction with either a network service or an applications service.

76. The general competition practices provided in the Act are new provisions. The purposes of these provisions are— — to provide protection for smaller providers in the absence of a general competitive policy or trade practices regulatory regime;

— to provide a context for, and certainty about, the manner in which the general powers and procedures under the Act should be administered. This reinforces the regulatory intent of the national policy for the development of the communications and multimedia industry; and — to establish a framework and clear powers for the Commission to ensure that anti-competitive practices do not undermine the national policy.

77. Part VI expressly—

— prohibits any conduct by any licensee who has the purpose of substantially lessening competition in a communications market;

— prohibits arrangements and practices such as rate fixing, market-sharing and boycotts; and

— prohibits mandatory tying or linking arrangements regarding the provision or supply of products and services.

78. The Act also provides for the Commission to publish guidelines which will apply when making assessments about "substantial lessening of competition".

79. Part VI also seeks to confer specific powers of direction on the Commission with respect to conduct by a licensee who is in a dominant position in a market. The Commission may determine that a licensee is in a dominant position in a market, and may publish guidelines which it will apply in making such determination. However, the Act makes no initial assumption that any licensee is in a dominant position in any communications market in Malaysia.

91. 92. Within this overall structure, the substantive aspects of technical regulation concern spectrum assignment, numbering and electronic addressing and the setting of technical codes and standards. These areas are discussed below.

Spectrum assignment:

93. Chapter 1 of this Part provides mechanisms to ensure the efficient assignment of frequency spectrum. Chapter 1 prohibits any

<!-- page 160 -->

person from using any part of the spectrum to supply a network service unless— — the person holds a spectrum assignment issued by the Commission under this Part; — the persons holds an apparatus assignment issued by the Commission under this Part; or — the use is subject to a class assignment issued by the Commission under this Part.

94. Assignments of spectrum under this Part are undertaken by the Commission. Any spectrum assignment, apparatus assignment or class assignment issued must be consistent with the spectrum plan developed by the Commission.

95. The Act provides for three types of assignments, namely, spectrum assignments, apparatus assignments and class assignments, but allows the Minister and the Commission flexibility in the administration of different types of assignments for different purposes: — a spectrum assignment authorises a person to use one or more specified frequency bands for any purpose consistent with the assignment conditions; — an apparatus assignment authorises a person to use one or more specified frequency bands to operate a network facility of a specified kind or for a specified purpose; — a class assignment authorises use by any person of a specified frequency band for a specified purpose.

While the Commission may assign rights to a person to use spectrum, the ultimate ownership of spectrum remains with the Government. In addition, the provision that the Act makes for tradeable rights for the use of spectrum is difficult to reconcile with the concept of a single licence, which cannot be subdivided.

97. It is envisaged that the Minister would determine spectrum to be assigned as a tradeable spectrum assignment in frequency bands if there is significant technological uncertainty about the ultimate use of the band. The secondary market in tradeable rights would subsequently allocate the band to its most efficient use.

## PART IX

119. Part IX of the Act seeks to provide a regulatory framework which is designed to promote— — the growth and development of content applications services reflecting Malaysia's cultural diversity; — the widespread availability of content applications services reflecting Malaysia's cultural diversity; and — adherence to community standards by content applications service providers.

120. It is intended that the regulatory measures under this Part will be administered in a manner that reflects their degree of influence in forming community views and standards. Hence the provisions of this Part allow the Minister and the Commission considerable flexibility in their application. This flexibility is necessary as, while it may be necessary to regulate certain content applications services which have a high degree of influence on a substantial proportion of the population, there are many content applications services which will require minimal, if any, direct regulation.

<!-- page 166 -->

121. The following diagram shows the major areas addressed under social regulation in the Act and the main areas of responsibility for both the Minister and the Commission:

122. Within this overall structure, the substantive aspects of social regulation seek to deal with licensing, content requirements and content codes. These areas are discussed below.

Licensing:

123. Part IX of the Act establishes a framework for the licensing of content applications service providers. Subject to such exemptions as may be determined by the Minister under the Act, no person may provide a content applications service unless the person holds an individual licence granted under this Part or the content applications service is subject to a class licence granted under this Part. A person who holds a licence under this Part is not exempted from the requirement to hold another licence in respect of other relevant activities under the Act.

124. The provision of content in the course of providing another service (that is, "content incidental to the service provided") is exempted from this Part.

125. The Minister may also determine guidelines to clarify the meaning of "content incidental to the service provided". This is to ensure that services which may unintentionally meet the definition of "content applications service" and which do not require regulation are exempted from the licensing requirements under this Part. An example of such a service is a World Wide Web site which uses a graphical interface (technically "content") to provide financial services.

<!-- page 167 -->

126. "Closed content applications services" are also exempted, and are initially defined as services confined to a dwelling or a body corporate. There is provision for the Minister to alter the definition at any time.

127. In addition to the above exemptions, a person who provides a limited content applications service, defined, in clause 6, as a service of limited appeal, is not required to hold an individual licence. The Minister may determine guidelines which clarify the meaning of "limited content applications service".

128. The Commission shall provide an opinion on the category of a limited content applications service upon request by a person.

129. The objective of these provisions is to ensure that content applications services which do not require extensive social regulation under this Part, because they are provided only to limited sectors of the population, are not subject to unnecessary regulation. A high level of regulation of these services is likely to impede the development of local content providers and therefore be inconsistent with the objects of the Act.

130. As with the other licensing provisions under the Act, the provisions for the licensing of the content applications service are not prescriptive with respect to the types of licences for different types of services.The absence of prescriptive licensing provisions in this Part is principally to allow flexibility with respect to the licensing of different types of content applications service providers depending on their degree of influence in forming community views and standards. For example, while a television broadcaster may be most appropriately subject to an individual licence, another content applications service provider whose services appeal to only a small class may only be subject to a class licence. There is an extremely wide range of content applications service providers, many of whom may not require any form of licensing at all.

Content requirements:

131. It will be an offence to provide content which is offensive, indecent, obscene, false or menacing. It is intended that codes addressing these matters will be developed under this Part.

132. The Commission may designate one or more industry forums under this Part in accordance with its general powers to designate an industry forum under Part V.

Matters which may be dealt with under a content code include, but are not limited to, the matters listed in the Act. In general, the intention of content codes is to restrict the provision of unsuitable content.

134. There may be multiple content forums and multiple content codes, as a content code may relate to— — a particular matter; — a particular type of content applications service provider; or — a particular matter for a particular type of content applications service provider.

135. The provisions relating to content codes under the Act allow flexibility in this respect because, while a content applications service provider who provides any content applications services which are of broad appeal to the general population may require reasonably prescriptive guidelines as to the type of content which may be provided, an applications service provider who provides any applications service which is of more limited appeal is unlikely to require as stringent regulation.

136. A content code is subject to the standard provisions relating to industry codes under Part V, including the provisions on mandatory standards.

## PART X

137. Part X seeks to deal with general provisions.

Installation of network facilities, access to network facilities, etc.:

138. Chapter 1 of Part X seeks to provide for the network facilities provider to enter on land and do anything that is necessary for his purposes.The provisions ensure that the provider must take all reasonable steps to ensure that he causes as little damage and inconvenience as is practicable. He is also under a duty to restore the land. The provider is required to enter into an agreement with a public utility if the activity undertaken by him is likely to affect the operations of the utility.The provider is further required to give written notice to the owner or occupier of the land before engaging in his activities on the land.The notice must state that if a person suffers financial loss or damage to property because of anything done by the provider, compensation may be payable.

139. Provision is made to require the provider to ensure that any network facilities installed over a road, bridge, path or navigable water is installed in a way that will allow reasonable passage by persons, vehicles and vessels.

140. The provider may apply to the Commission for a network facilities installation permit authorising him to carry out the installation of the facilities. Amongst other things, the provider must satisfy the Commission that he has made reasonable efforts to negotiate in good faith with each proprietor whose approval is required for carrying out the installation and that at least one of those approvals has not been obtained within fourteen days after the beginning of the negotiations concerned.The necessary approval of the relevant State Authority, local authority or other authority must be obtained first.

141. Notwithstanding the provisions of any other written law, a network facilities provider or a public utility shall provide a network facilities provider with non-discriminatory access to any post, network facilities or right-of-way owned or controlled by him.

Additional offences and penalties:

142. Chapter 2 of Part X seeks to provide for additional offences and penalties not already dealt with in the Act. Amongst other things, the Act makes it an offence for a person to intercept communications without a legal authorisation, or to fraudulently use or obtain any network facilities, network service, applications service or content applications service. Adequate provisions are made to include all cases of "cloning" of customer equipment or other device to gain unauthorised access to services, and offences relating to the tampering with or the use of equipment or software in order to defraud.

Powers of entry, investigation into offences and prosecution:

143. Chapter 3 of Part X seeks to deal with the powers of entry of authorised officers, investigation into offences and prosecution.

144. The Sessions Court shall have jurisdiction to try offences under the Act.

National interest matters:

145. Part X of the Act also sets out various obligations regarding the protection of national interest matters which may be imposed on licensees.These include provisions for Government control of communications in an emergency, and disaster recovery plans.

147. Part XI of the Act seeks to establish arrangements and procedures for the smooth transition from the current regulatory regime to the new regime under the Act. The transitional provisions are summarised in the diagram below:

148. Part XI repeals the Telecommunications Act 1950 and the Broadcasting Act 1988 ("repealed Acts") from the appointed date, that is, the date of coming into operation of the Act. The Act will replace the provisions of these two earlier Acts.

149. The Act preserves any subsidiary legislation made under the repealed Acts until new subsidiary legislation are made to replace them.The Minister is empowered to make the necessary modifications to such subsidiary legislation for the purposes of removing difficulties or in consequence of the passing of the Act.

150. A person who holds an existing licence granted under the repealed Acts may apply to the Commission for a new licence under the Act. An old licence shall only be effective for twelve months after the appointed date. The Minister may grant a new licence which may be subject to any conditions that the Minister considers are in the national interest.

151. If a licence granted under a repealed Act conflicts with a class licence granted under the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998, the licence granted under the repealed Acts shall have no effect to the extent of the conflict.

152. A person who wishes to continue to operate under a licence granted under the repealed Acts must register it with the Commission within twelve months of the new Act coming into operation.

153. A person who is holding a licence granted under the repealed Acts after the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 comes into operation must indicate to the Minister in writing as to whether he intends to seek a new licence under the new Act within twelve months from the appointed date.

154. Persons operating under existing licences granted under the repealed Acts are not entitled to receive any new benefits for which the licensees may be eligible under the Act. 155. The transitional provisions relating to licensing are intended to allow the Minister and the Commission to establish incentives for existing licensees to move to new licences under the new licensing regime. Such incentives may involve the application of other provisions under the Act, including the general licensing provisions and provisions on the universal service provision system.The incentives offered to licensees should benefit the licensee as well as assist to further the objects of the Act.The intention is that no licensee should be any worse off under a new licence. The provisions ensure this by allowing the existing licensees to choose whether and when (but not later than twelve months) they wish to migrate to a new licence.
