## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Income Tax Act 1967, the Petroleum (Income Tax) Act 1967 and the Stamp Act 1949.

## CHAPTER II

## AMENDMENTS TO THE INCOME TAX ACT 1967

The Income Tax Act 1967 is referred to in this Chapter as "Act 53".

2. Clause 5 of the Bill seeks to amend section 46 of Act 53 to provide for an increase in the personal deduction allowed for an individual or a Hindu joint family from four thousand ringgit to eight thousand ringgit. In the case of an individual, the personal deduction is given to the individual in respect of himself and his dependent relatives (if any). This amendment is effective from the year of assessment 2000 on a current year basis.

<!-- page 16 -->

3. Clause 6 of the Bill seeks to amend section 49 of Act 53. Subsection 49(1B) is amended to provide that the deduction of two thousand ringgit given to an individual for any premium paid by him for the purchase of insurance on education or for medical benefits is to be increased to three thousand ringgit.

A new subsection 49(1c) is introduced to provide that a deduction of one thousand ringgit is to be given to an individual for any premium paid by him for the purchase of an insurance policy determined by the Employees Provident Fund Board utilizing the amount standing to his credit in the Employees Provident Fund. If subsection 50(2) of Act 53 applies, a further deduction of one thousand ringgit is to be given but if the wife has no total income, the total deduction allowed under this subsection shall not exceed one thousand ringgit.

This amendment is effective from the year of assessment 2000 on a current year basis.

4. Clause 8 of the Bill seeks to delete section 60D of Act 53. It is proposed to exempt venture capital companies from income tax by orders made under section 12iof Act 53. This amendment is effective from the year of assessment 2000 on a current year basis.

5. Clause 9 of the Bill seeks to amend section 101 of Act 53. The proposed amendment seeks to require the Director General to carry out a review of an assessment within twelve months from the date of receipt of the notice of appeal or within such extended period as may be granted by the Minister, which shall not exceed six months from the date of expiry of the twelve-month period. This amendment is effective upon the coming into operation of the proposed Act.

6. Clause 10 of the Bill seeks to amend section 102 of Act 53 to require the Director General to send an appeal forward to the Special Commissioners within twelve months from the date of receipt of the notice of appeal or within such extended period as may be granted by the Minister if the Director General is of the opinion that there is no reasonable prospect of settling the matter in accordance with section 101 of Act 53. This amendment is effective upon the coming into operation of the proposed Act.

7. Clause 11 of the Bill seeks to amend section 107B of Act 53. The proviso to subsection 107B(2) is amended to provide that a person who is required to make payment by instalments on account of tax may apply to the Director General not later than the thirtieth day of June to vary the amount of instalments and the number of instalments. Before this proposed amendment, a person was allowed to do so before the fifteenth day of April. This amendment is effective from the year of assessment 2000 on a current year basis.

8. Clause 12 of the Bill seeks to amend Parts I and IV of Schedule 1 to Act

53. The proposed amendment to Part I of Schedule 1 seeks to provide for the rates of tax in respect of resident persons to range from 0 to 29 per cent. This

<!-- page 17 -->

represents a reduction in the existing rates of tax in respect of resident persons. In relation to non-resident persons other than companies, the rate of tax is reduced from 30 per cent to 29 per cent. The proposed amendment to Part IV of Schedule 1 is to reduce the existing rates of tax for co-operative societies which range from 0 to 30 per cent to range from 0 to 29 per cent. This amendment is effective from the year of assessment 2000 on a current year basis.

9. Clause 13 of the Bill seeks to amend Schedule 3 to Act 53 by introducing new paragraphs 16A and 67B into Schedule 3. Paragraph 16A provides that a person who has incurred qualifying building expenditure on the construction of a building to which paragraph 67B applies where at the end of the basis period for a year of assessment the building was on lease to the Government is to be given an allowance equal to three-fiftieths or such other prescribed fraction of that expenditure.

Paragraph 67B provides that a building constructed on a build-lease-transfer basis pursuant to an agreement entered into between a person and the Government shall, subject to the approval of the Minister of Finance, be treated as an industrial building for the purposes of giving the allowance.

This amendment is effective from the year of assessment 2000 on a preceding year basis.

10. Clause 14 of the Bill seeks to introduce a new paragraph 42A into Schedule 5 to Act 53 to provide for the adoption of the procedure and practice in force or in use in the subordinate court and the High Court if such procedure or practice is not expressly provided for in Schedule 5. This amendment is effective upon the coming into operation of the proposed Act.

11. Clause 15 of the Bill seeks to introduce a new paragraph 320 into Schedule 6 to Act 53 to provide for an exemption in respect of income earned for musical compositions up to an amount of RM20,000. This amendment is effective from the year of assessment 2000 on a current year basis.

## CHAPTER III

## AMENDMENTS TO THE PETROLEUM (INCOME TAX) ACT 1967

The Petroleum (Income Tax) Act 1967 is referred to in this Chapter as "Act 543".

12. Clause 17 of the Bill seeks to amend section 13A of Act 543 to provide that the disposal value of a vested asset shall not be treated as gross income of Petroleum Nasional Berhad or the Malaysia-Thailand Joint Authority where the vested asset that has been disposed of continues to be used for petroleum operations by a chargeable person under another petroleum agreement and that chargeable person has not incurred qualifying expenditure in respect of that asset. This amendment is effective from the year of assessment 2000.

<!-- page 18 -->

13. Clause 18 of the Bill seeks to amend the Second Schedule to Act 543. Paragraphs 8, 12, 13 and 14 of the Second Schedule are amended to enable the Minister of Finance to prescribe the rates for initial allowances in respect of plant and machinery as well as annual allowances for plant, machinery, fixed off-shore platforms and industrial buildings. These amendments shall be deemed to have been effective from the year of assessment 1996.

Paragraphs 21A and 23A are introduced into the Second Schedule to Act 543 to provide for the transfer of the residual expenditure of a vested asset under the first petroleum agreement to the second petroleum agreement where-

(a) the relationship specified in subsubparagraph 21A(a), (b), (c) or (d) exists between the disposer and the acquirer of a vested asset;

(b) the vested asset continues to be used for petroleum operations by a chargeable person under the second petroleum agreement; and

(c) the chargeable person has not incurred qualifying expenditure in respect of the vested asset.

This amendment is effective from the year of assessment 2000.

Subsubparagraph 41(b) of the Second Schedule to Act 543 is amended to provide that the disposer of a vested asset shall not be entitled to a claim for balancing allowance. This amendment is effective from the year of assessment

2000.

## CHAPTER IV

## AMENDMENTS TO THE STAMP ACT 1949

The Stamp Act 1949 is referred to in this Chapter as "Act 378".

14. Clause 20 of the Bill seeks to amend the First Schedule to Act 378 by introducing a new item 49A into the First Schedule to provide that a lease or an agreement for a lease under the principles of Al-Ijarah of the Syariah law for the purpose of financing or securing repayment of money will be charged the same duty that is chargeable on an instrument of security under item 27 of that Schedule. This amendment shall be deemed to have come into operation on 30 October 1999.

15. Clause 21 of the Bill seeks to amend the Second Schedule to Act 378 by introducing a new item 19 into the Second Schedule to provide that an immigration officer is required to cancel the adhesive stamps on security bonds for the grant of a Professional Visit Pass for an Artiste, a Social Visit Pass and a Work Permit Pass. This amendment is effective upon the coming into operation of the proposed Act.
