### New section 4A

5. The principal Act is amended by inserting after section 4 the following section:

### "Offence of promoting feelings of ill-will or hostility

4A. (1) Any person who, before, during or after an election, directly or indirectly, by himself or by any other person on his behalf, does any act or makes any statement with a view or with a tendency to promote feelings of ill-will, discontent or hostility between persons of the same race or different races or of the same class or different classes of the population of Malaysia in order to induce any elector or voter to vote or refrain from voting at an election or to procure or endeavour to procure the election of any person shall be liable, on conviction, . to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to a fine not exceeding ten thousand ringgit or to both such imprisonment and fine.

(2) Subject to any specific provision to the contrary in any written law relating to an election, any person who is convicted of an offence under this section shall, until the expiration of five years from such conviction, be incapable of being registered or listed as an elector or of voting at an election or of being elected at an election, and if at that date he has been elected at an election, his seat shall be vacated from the date of such conviction.".

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Election Offences Act 1954 ("Act 5").

2. Clause 1 seeks to provide for the short title and contains provisions on the commencement of the proposed Act.

3. Clause 2 seeks to amend section 2 of Act 5. It seeks to introduce the definition of "campaign period" which refers to the period in which a candidate or his election agent may hold election campaign in his constituency. The definition of "returning officer" is introduced for the purpose of clarification only.

4. Clause 3 seeks to amend section 3 of Act 5 relating to electoral offences. It seeks to introduce two new offences, namely the offence of obstructing or preventing a voter who is entitled to vote from voting at an election and the offence of voting in an election at more than one polling station whether in the same or different constituency. It also seeks to enhance the existing penalty of a fine of two thousand ringgit to five thousand ringgit.

5. tClause 4 seeks to amend section 4 of Act 5, primarily to enhance the existing penalty of a fine of two thousand ringgit to five thousand ringgit.

6. Clause 5 seeks to introduce a new section 4A into Act 5. The new section 4A is essentially a restatement of the provisions of paragraph 3(l)(e) of the Sedition Act 1948, incorporated with a view to preserving racial unity, etc. by prohibiting categorically any person from doing any act or making any statement which may create feelings of ill-will, discontent or hostility amongst the population of Malaysia in his endeavours to procure the election of any person.

7. Clause 6 seeks to amend section 5 of Act 5 in relation to the penalty prescribed in subsection (7). The maximum term of imprisonment is now enhanced to one year from the existing six months while the maximum fine of five hundred ringgit is enhanced to three thousand ringgit.

8. Clause JO seeks to amend section 11 of Act 5. It proposes to enhance the penalties which can be imposed for corrupt practices to ensure that the penalties are commensurate with such offences. The maximum term of imprisonment of twelve months is now enhanced to two years and the maximum fine of one thousand ringgit is enhanced to five thousand ringgit. The maximum term of imprisonment of six months is enhanced to one year while the maximum fine is enhanced to two thousand ringgit from the existing five hundred ringgit.

9. Clause 11 seeks to amend subsection 12(6A) of Act 5. This amendment is consequential to the amendment made in regulation 4 of the Elections (Conduct of Elections) Regulations 1981 which requires only a candidate's correspondence address to be stated in his nomination papers.
