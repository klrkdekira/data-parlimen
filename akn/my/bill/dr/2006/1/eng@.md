intituled

An Act to amend the Factories and Machinery Act 1967.

ENACTED by the Parliament of Malaysia as follows:

### Short title and commencement

1. (1) This Act may be cited as the Factories and Machinery (Amendment) Act 2006.

(2) This Act comes into operation on a date to be appointed by the Minister by notification in the Gazette.

7D. ( 1 ) Notwithstanding any other provisions of this Act, the Minister shall have the power to grant a licence on such conditions as he may think fit to any person to perform any of the functions specified in this Act as the functions of a licensed person as it appears to him to be necessary.

### Revocation of licence

7E. (1) If the Chief Inspector is satisfied that a licensed person has breached any condition of the licence, the Chief Inspector may give a notice in writing to the licensed person requiring him to comply with the condition which he has breached and take all measures stipulated therein within the period stated in the notice.

(2) If by the time the period stipulated in the notice expires the licensed person has failed to comply with the notice, the Chief Inspector shall present a report to the Minister on such failure.

(3) If, after considering the report from the Chief Inspector, the Minister is satisfied that the breach is serious in nature or affects or may affect the safety and health of workers or members of the public and that the licensed person has failed or refused to take or has not taken all measures stipulated in the notice for the purpose of ensuring compliance with the condition which the licensed person has breached, the Minister

<!-- page 10 -->

may give the licensed person a notice stating that he proposes to revoke the licence given to such person and the notice shall also state —

(a) the breach alleged against the licensed person and the actions or omissions that constitute the breach; and

(b) the period (which must not be less than thirty days from the date of the notice) within which the licensed person may make representations pertaining to the revocation.

(4) After the expiry of the period stated in the notice and after considering any representations made by the licensed person, the Minister may decide whether to continue with the proposed revocation or take no further action.

(5) Notice shall be given to the licensed person by the Minister informing him of the decision where the Minister decides to revoke the licence or take no further action, and the decision shall come into force on the date the notice is delivered to the licensed person.

(6) The decision of the Minister under this section is final.

(7) Notwithstanding the revocation of the licence, the validity of any certificate of fitness issued by the licensed person shall continue to be valid until the expiry date thereof and the licenced person shall continue to assume any obligation, duty and responsibility imposed by or in connection with the issuance of the certificate of fitness.

EXPLANATORY STATEMENT This Bill seeks to

amend the Factories and Machinery Act 1967 ("Act 139").

2. Clause J contains the short title and the power of the Minister to appoint the commencement date of the proposed Act,

<!-- page 25 -->

3. Clause 2 seeks to amend section 2 of Act 139 to include pipelines in the definition of factory.

4. Clause 3 seeks to amend section 3 of Act 139 to include pipelines, fired pressure vessels, materials handling equipment, amusement devices and equipment for the cutting of metals in the definition of "machinery". This will have the effect of expanding the scope of Act 139 to include such items.

5. Clause 4 seeks to provide for the delegation of powers or duties by the Chief Inspector to any of the Deputy Chief Inspector, Senior Inspector or Inspector.

6. Clause 5 seeks to amend section 6 of Act 139 to deem a licensed person, his servants and agents to be public servants within the meaning of the Penal Code.

7. Clause 6 seeks to introduce new sections 7A, 7B, 7C, 7D, 7E and 7F into Act

139. The new sections 7A, 7B and 7c deal with the power of the inspector to enter premises with or without a search warrant and the power of seizure while the new section 7D deals with the appointment, power and duties of a licensed person. The new section 7E provides for the revocation of licence if a licensed person breaches any condition of the licence and section 7F allows the Minister to grant a new licence upon such revocation.

8. Clause 7 seeks to amend section 8 of Act 139 to widen the scope of the offence of obstruction to include the obstruction of a licensed person and to enhance the existing maximum fine which can be imposed from five thousand ringgit to two hundred thousand ringgit.

9. Clause 8 seeks to amend section 9 of Act 139 to prohibit the disclosure of manufacturing or commercial secret obtained in the course of duty, except under certain circumstances.

10. Clause 9 seeks to introduce new subsection 19(4) into Act 139 to provide for termination of the certificate of fitness of any machinery which is being dismantled or repaired or damaged.

11. Clause 10 seeks to introduce a new section 19A into Act 139. The proposed section seeks to empower the Chief Inspector to revoke any certificate of fitness obtain by way of fraud or misrepresentation.

12. Clause 12 seeks to introduce anew section 29A into Act 139. The proposed section prohibits the manufacture, testing, installation or repair of any machinery which is prescribed unless there is a written authority issued by the Chief Inspector.

13. Clause 13 seeks to amend section 31 of Act 139 to require an accident or dangerous occurrence which causes serious bodily injury to be reported to the Inspector having jurisdiction for the area in which the accident or dangerous occurrence takes place.

<!-- page 26 -->

14. Clause 14 seeks to amend section 32 of Act 139 to replace the term "industrial diseases" with the term "occupational diseases" since the latter is the more appropriate term now.

15. Clause 15 seeks to amend section 33 of Act 139 so that preliminary investigation is required only in cases where an accident has caused loss of life to any person or serious damage to machinery or other property.

16. Clause 16 seeks to amend section 36 of Act 139 to require a written notice to be served on an Inspector or a licensed person whenever any machinery in respect of which a certificate of fitness is prescribed is installed and the Inspector or the licensed person will make an inspection of the machinery. If upon inspection, certificate of fitness is refused, the aggrieved person may appeal to the Chief Inspector for a re-inspection.

17. Clause 17 seeks to amend section 37 of Act 139 to require any person who is in possession of any machinery in respect of which a certificate of fitness is prescribed to send particulars relating to the machinery to a licensed person, apart from an Inspector.

18. Clause 18 proposes a new subsection 38(4) which gives a licensed person access to information in the general register or other register pertaining to machinery in respect of which a certificate of fitness is prescribed.

19. Clause 19 seeks to amend section 40 of Act 139 to empower a licensed person to inspect machinery in respect of which a certificate of fitness is prescribed. This clause also proposes a new subsection 40(2A) which requires the occupier of a factory to provide an Inspector and a licensed person all reasonable facilities for the inspection and testing of machinery which is in the occupier's possession.

20. Clause 20 seeks to amend subsection 41(1) of Act 139 to allow appeals to be made from the decision of a licensed person, and to introduce a new paragraph 4l(4)(da) to provide for the finality of the decision of the Board of Appeal.

21. Clause 21 seeks to amend section 44 of Act 139 to require that notice under subsections 44(1) and 44(2) shall also be given to a licensed person.

22. Clause 22 seeks to introduce a new section 47A into Act 139 to provide for protection to an Inspector and a licensed person against personal liability for any loss or damage caused when carrying out their duties, unless it was caused intentionally or through recklessness or negligence.

23. Clause 23 seeks to amend section 48 of Act 139 so that a licensed person will be able to recover any fees payable to him as a debt due to him.

24. Clause 24 seeks to amend section 51 of Act 139 to enhance the existing maximum fines in subsections 51(1), (2) and (3). This clause also proposes a new subsection 51(4) which makes the failure to comply with any written order or notice under subsection 39(3) or 40(4) of the Act an offence.

25. Clause 25 seeks to introduce a new section 51A into Act 139 to empower a Court of a First Class Magistrate to have jurisdiction to try and impose the full punishment for any offence under the Act.

26. Clause 26 seeks to amend section 52 of Act 139 on the institution of prosecution under the Act so that it is in line with Article 145 of the Federal Constitution.

27. Clause 27 seeks to amend section 52A of Act 139 to spell out the procedure for the compounding of offences under the Act.

28. Clause 29 seeks to amend section 56 of Act 139 which deals with the power of the Minister to make regulations.

29. Other amendments not specifically referred to in this Statement are minor and consequential in nature.

This Bill will not involve the Government in any extra financial expenditure.
