## A BILL

intituled

An Act to amend the National Land Code. [ ]

WHEREAS it is expedient for the purposes only of ensuring uniformity of law and policy that the National Land Code be amended:

NOW, THEREFORE, pursuant to Clause (4) of Article 76 of the Federal Constitution, IT IS ENACTED by the Parliament of Malaysia as follows:

### Short title and commencement

1. (1) This Act may be cited as the National Land Code (Amendment) Act 2007.

(2) Subject to subsection (4), this Act comes into operation in each State on a date to be appointed by the Minister, with the approval of the National Land Council, by notification in the Gazette of the Federation, and the Minister may, with the approval of the National Land Council, appoint different dates in respect of different provisions of this Act.

(3) Subject to subsection (4), this Act comes into operation in the Federal Territories of Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya on such date as the Minister may, by notification in the Gazette of the Federation, and the Minister may appoint different dates in respect of different provisions of this Act.

(4) Sections 10 and 21 are deemed to have come into operation on 25 March 1985 and 1 December 2001 respectively.

## ELECTRONIC LAND ADMINISTRATION SYSTEM

### Interpretation

1. In this Schedule, unless the context otherwise requires—

“computer” means any device for storing or processing information and includes the necessary input and output and database of a computer;

“computer document of licence or permit” means any computer printed document of licence or computer printed document of permit prepared by use of a computer;

“computer document of title” means any computer register document of title or computer issue document of title stored virtually in the land database, and includes print out thereof;

“digital data” means all land related information recorded into land database by electronic technology that generates, stores, preserves and processes data in terms of a computer file containing land titles, images, documents or spatial and textual data, in which the outputs are available in computerized formats upon the coming into force of the Electronic Land Administration System;

“digital signature” means a transformation of a message using an asymmetric cryptosystem such that a person having the initial message and the signer’s public key can accurately determine—

(a) whether the transformation was created using the private key that corresponds to the signer’s public key; and

(b) whether the message has been altered since the transformation was made;

“disaster”, in the context of the Electronic Land Administration System, means any natural or man-made disaster or hardware failure that causes an extended service interruption to the data processing services which cannot be corrected within an acceptable time which necessitates the use of an alternate site or equipment for recovery;

“Electronic Land Administration System” means an integrated electronic processing of applications on land matters, registration of titles, documents required to be prepared thereby and any entry in any document of title through the use of a computer which includes both manual and computerized procedures;

<!-- page 15 -->

“entry”, in relation to any document of title, includes any endorsement, memorial, note, correction, cancellation or deletion or any other entry thereon by use of a computer;

“existing document of title” means any register document of title or issue document of title issued manually or by use of a computer registered before or after the commencement of the Electronic Land Administration System;

“land database” means a resource of information that contains all kinds of digital data or computer registers of titles or any other land related information, electronically stored in computerized formats upon the coming into force of the Electronic Land Administration System in a land Registry;

“matter” means any instrument presented for registration or any order, notification, application or other document presented for entry into the register.

### Mode of making an entry on a document of title under the Electronic

### Land Administration System

2. For the purposes of this Act, whenever an entry is required to be made on the computer document of title the Registrar shall—

(a) make the entry; and

(b) prepare a new computer document of title and shall cancel and destroy the previous version of the computer document of title.

### Forms for Registry title

3. For the purpose of section 86, in the case of land to be alienated under Registry title—

(a) the computer register document of title shall consist of a grant in Form 5Be or a State lease in Form 5Ce, according as the land is to be alienated in perpetuity or for a term of years; and

(b) the computer issue document of title shall be in the same form as Form 5Be or Form 5Ce, as the case may be.

### Forms of documents for Land Office title

4. For the purpose of section 87, in the case of land to be alienated under Land Office title—

(a) the computer register document of title shall consist of a Mukim grant in Form 5De or a Mukim lease in Form 5Ee, according as the land is to be alienated in perpetuity or for a term of years; and

(b) the computer issue document of title shall be in the same form as Form 5De or Form 5Ee, as the case may be.

### Forms of documents for qualified titles

5. For the purpose of subsection 177(2), documents of qualified titles shall consist of—

(a) the computer register document of title in Form 11Ae or 11Be, according as the land in question is to be held under the form of qualified title corresponding to Registry title or that corresponding to Land Office title; and

### Plan for computer document of title

6. (1) The plan of the land to be alienated under final title shall—

(a) in the case of land held under Registry title, be issued by the Registrar in Form Ble separately to the proprietor and he shall duly authenticate it under his digital signature; or

(a) in the case of land held under Registry title, be issued by the Registrar in Form B2e separately to the proprietor and he shall duly authenticate it under his digital signature; or

### Conversion to computer register document of title

7. (1) Upon the coming into force of the Electronic Land Administration System in a land Registry, the Registrar shall convert the existing register documents of title, to the computer documents of title and shall authenticate the same under his digital signature.

(2) An existing register documents of title shall continue to be in force and valid for all purposes of the Code until a computer documents of title is prepared and duly authenticated by the Registrar.

(3) After the conversion of an existing register document of title to the computer document of title, the existing issue documents of title in respect thereof shall continue to be in force and valid for all purposes of this Code until the relevant computer issue document of title is prepared and issued to the proprietor.

(4) The computer documents of title to be prepared in respect of an existing printed document of title prepared under this Code shall be in Forms 5Be, 5Ce, 5De, 5Ee, 1lAe or 1lBe of this Schedule, as the case may be.

(5) In respect of an existing printed document of title registered under the previous land law, the computer document of title shall be deemed to be a process for the preparation of titles in continuation.

(6) Upon the conversion of an existing document of title to a computer document of title under the preceding subparagraphs, the Registrar shall—

(a) enter the plan into Form B1e or B2e as the case may be, and duly authenticate it under his digital signature;

8. (1) Every document of title to be prepared upon alienation of land or title in continuation or replacement of a computer issue document of title, under the Electronic Land Administration System, shall be prepared by the use of a computer.

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the National Land Code 1965 (“the Code”) which includes the insertion of a new Sixteenth Schedule to enable the introduction and the usage of an electronic land administration system in Peninsular Malaysia.

2. Clause 1 contains the short title and provisions on the commencement of the proposed Act.

3. Clause 2 seeks to amend section 5 of the Code to introduce new definitions and to amend existing definitions of certain terms used in the Code consequent upon amendments proposed in the Bill.

4. Clause 3 seeks to amend section 5B of the Code to empower the Minister to make any amendments to the Fourteenth Schedule. The present provision only enables the Minister to amend the forms in that Schedule.

5. Clause 4 seeks to introduce a new PART ONE (C) into the Code which will facilitate the implementation of the Electronic Land Administration System. Among others, it empowers the Minister to appoint different dates for the coming into operation of the Electronic Land Administration System in any land Registry. The Minister is also empowered to make any amendments to the Sixteenth Schedule by an order.

6. Clause 5 seeks to amend paragraph 14(1)(i) of the Code to enable the State Authority to appoint collecting agents to accept payment for any item of land revenue.

7. Clause 6 seeks to amend paragraph 62(2)(c) of the Code to clarify that the officers appointed to have the control of any reserved land shall be public officers.

8. Clause 7 seeks to amend subsection 80(3) of the Code to enable the Registrar to issue a final title in respect of a land without first issuing a qualified title.

9. Clause 8 seeks to amend paragraph 81(1)(d) of the Code to provide for fees chargeable in connection with the preparation and registration of documents of qualified title and final document of title or final document of title.

10. Clause 9 seeks to amend section 92D of the Code to clarify that the State Authority may entertain an application from the proprietor of any alienated land for the independent use or alienation of underground land below the alienated land only after any rent payable in respect of the land has been paid.

11. Clause 10 seeks to amend paragraph 124(4)(c) to insert the words “or restriction in interest”. These words should have been incorporated when amendments were made to section 124 vide Act A587 which came into operation on 25 March 1985. This clause is proposed to be given a retrospective effect to that date.

<!-- page 47 -->

12. Clause 11 seeks to introduce a new paragraph (ca) into subsection 136(1) of the Code. The new paragraph creates a new condition that must be satisfied before an application for subdivision is approved, namely, the proprietor of the land has agreed to surrender such part of the land to be used for public purposes, the use of which has been approved by the appropriate planning authority.

13. Clause 12 seeks to introduce a new subsection (3) into section 140 of the Code to enable the co-proprietors of alienated land to partition the land which is subject to the category of “agriculture” or to any condition requiring its use for an agricultural purpose. The said land is two-fifths of a hectare, more or less than two-fifths of a hectare. The application for partition which is less than two-fifths of a hectare requires approval by the State Authority.

14. Clause 13 seeks to amend section 141 of the Code consequent upon the amendment made to section 140.

15. Clause 14 seeks to replace section 141A of the Code. The new section 141A enables any co-proprietor to apply for partition of the land without having to take into consideration the proportion of his share in the land.

16. Clause 15 seeks to introduce a new subsection (5) into section 142 which relates to the submission by the Land Administrator to the State Authority of any application to partition land made pursuant to section 143A.

17. Clause 16 seeks to introduce a new section 143A into the Code which will enable the State Authority to entertain applications for partition of agriculture land subject to restrictions under subparagraph 136(1)(f)(i) of the Code and provides the procedure thereof.

18. Clause 17 seeks to amend section 147 of the Code consequent upon the amendment made to subsection 136(1).

19. Clause 18 seeks to introduce a new subsection (1A) into section 204GA which allows the Land Administrator to cancel or cause to be cancelled the note of the chargee’s consent on the register document of title in respect of any land where the proprietor withdraws his application for the surrender and re-alienation of the land or the State Authority rejects it.

20. Clause 19 seeks to amend subsection 254(1) of the Code to provide for an alternative minimum period of not less than one month before a chargee could serve on the chargor a notice in Form 16D for any breach of agreement.

21. Clause 20 seeks to amend subsection 264A(1) of the Code to correct a printing error. Form 16P should have been read as Form 16O when section 264A was first introduced into the Code vide Act A1104 which came into operation on 1 December 2001. This clause is also proposed to be given a retrospective effect to that date.

22. Clause 21 seeks to amend subsection 375(2) of the Code to also impose on the Registrar the responsibility for the safe keeping of digital data and information stored in the land database at the land Registry and the Disaster Recovery Centre.

<!-- page 48 -->

23. Clause 22 seeks to amend subsection 399(1) of the Code to state that the duty of the Director of Survey and Mapping is also to safely keep all survey records including the digital cadastral database.

24. Clause 23 seeks to amend paragraph 412(1)(a) of the Code to state that copies of deposited plans may also be produced from the digital cadastral database.

25. Clause 24 seeks to amend section 426 of the Code to increase the penalty for extraction or removal of rock material from a fine of ten thousand ringgit to fifty thousand ringgit and the imprisonment term from one year to five years.

26. Clause 25 seeks to amend section 429A to provide that the prosecution of offences under the Code must be with the consent in writing of the Public Prosecutor.

27. Clause 26 seeks to substitute section 429B of the Code which deals with compounding of offences. Power to compound by the State Director or Land Administrator is subject to the consent of the Public Prosecutor.

28. Clauses 27 and 28 seek to amend the First Schedule and the Fourteenth Schedule of the Code respectively. The amendments to the First and Fourteenth Schedules are for clarification purposes.

29. Clause 29 seeks to introduce a new Sixteenth Schedule into the Code containing detailed provisions relating to the implementation of the Electronic Land Administration System.

30. Other amendments not specifically dealt with in this Statement are minor or consequential in nature.
