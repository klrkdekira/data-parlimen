## PART I

## PRELIMINARY

### Short title, commencement and application

1.(1) This Act may be cited as the Special Complaints Commission Act 2007.

(2) This Act comes into operation on a date to be appointed by the Prime Minister by notification in the Gazette.

(3) This Act applies to complaints lodged with the Commission with respect to misconduct committed by any enforcement officer on or after the commencement of this Act.

(4) This Act shall not apply to complaints with respect to any conduct performed outside the scope of official duties of an enforcement officer as conferred by the law.

<!-- page 5 -->

### Interpretation

2. In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires —

"enforcement agency" means a Federal body or agency or statutory body that is for the time being responsible in Malaysia for the enforcement of laws relating to the prevention, detection and investigation of any offence;

"statutory body" means the statutory body itself or, where the incorporating law provides for a separate board, council or committee by whatever name called to carry out the functions of the statutory body and to have charge of the general management and administration of the statutory body, that board, council or committee;

"conveyance" means any vehicle, vessel, ship, aircraft or any other mode of transportation whether by air, sea or land;

"Task Force" means the Task Force established under section 27;

"enforcement officer" means an officer—

(a) of the enforcement agency; or

(b) of the joint service established under Article 133 of the Federal Constitution to carry out enforcement power;

"premises" includes house, building, land, easement of any tenure, whether open or enclosed, whether built on or not, whether public or private, and whether maintained or not under statutory authority;

"Disciplinary Authority", in relation to an enforcement officer in —

(a) a Federal Ministry, means the appropriate Disciplinary Authority established under Part X of the Federal Constitution but does not include the Armed Forces Council established under Article 137 of the Federal Constitution;

## PART II

## ESTABLISHMENT OF THE SPECIAL COMPLAINTS COMMISSION

### Establishment of the Commission

3. (1) There shall be established for the purposes of this Act a body corporate to be known as the Special Complaints Commission with perpetual succession and a common seal, and which may sue and be sued in its corporate name.

(2) The Commission may enter into contracts and may acquire, purchase, take, hold and enjoy movable and immovable property of every description and may convey, assign, surrender, yield up, charge, mortgage, demise, reassign, transfer, or otherwise disposes of, or deal with, any movable or immovable property or any interest therein vested in the Commission upon such terms as it deems fit.

### Constitution of the Commission

4. (1) The Commission shall consist of the following members:

(a) a Chairman to be appointed by the Prime Minister;

<!-- page 7 -->

(b) the Inspector General of Police;

(c) the Director General of Public Complaints Bureau;

(d) the Director General of the Anti-Corruption Agency; and

(e) three other members appointed by the Prime Minister.

(2) The appointment of members of the Commission shall be published in the Gazette.

### Tenure of office

5. (1) A member of the Commission appointed under paragraphs 4(l)(a) and (e) shall hold office for a term not exceeding two years.

(2) Such member of the Commission shall be eligible for reappointment but no such member shall hold office for more than two consecutive terms.

### Allowances

6. The members of the Commission shall be paid such allowances as the Prime Minister may determine.

### Temporary exercise of functions of Chairman

7. (1) The Prime Minister may appoint temporarily any member of the Commission to act as the Chairman for the period when —

(a) the office of the Chairman is vacant;

(b) the Chairman is absent from duty or from Malaysia; or

(c) the Chairman is, for any other reason, unable to perform his functions.

(2) A member appointed under subsection (1) shall, during the period in which he is performing the function of the Chairman under this section, be deemed to be the Chairman.

<!-- page 8 -->

### Revocation of appointment and resignation

8. (1) The appointment of any member appointed under paragraph 4(l)(a) or (e) may at any time be revoked by the Prime Minister without assigning any reason therefor.

(2) A member appointed under paragraph 4(l)(a) or (e) may at any time resign his office by a written notice addressed to the Prime Minister.

### Vacation of office

9. The office of a member of the Commission appointed under section 4 shall be vacated if—

(a) he dies;

(b) there has been proved against him, or he has been convicted of, a charge in respect of—

(i) an offence involving fraud, dishonesty or moral turpitude; (ii) an offence under any law relating to corruption; or (iii) any other offence punishable with imprisonment (in itself only or in addition to or in lieu of a fine) for more than two years;

(c) he becomes a bankrupt;

(d) he is of unsound mind or is otherwise incapable of discharging his duties;

(e) he absents himself from three consecutive meetings of the Commission without leave of the Chairman or, in the case of the Chairman, without leave of the Prime Minister;

(f) his resignation is accepted by the Prime Minister; or

(g) his appointment is revoked by the Prime Minister.

### Protection from personal liability

10. No member of the Commission shall incur personal liability for any loss or damage caused by any act or omission in administering the affairs of the Commission, unless the loss

<!-- page 9 -->

or damage is occasioned by an intentionally wrongful act or omission on his part.

### Application of Schedule

11. (1) The Schedule applies to members of the Commission.

(2) The Prime Minister may amend the Schedule by order published in the Gazette.

### Delegation of functions and powers

12. (1) The Commission may, subject to such conditions, limitations or restrictions as it deems fit to impose, delegate any of its functions and powers, to —

(a) the Chairman;

(b) any member of the Commission; or

(c) the Task Force.

(2) Any person or the Task Force delegated with such functions and powers shall observe and have regard to all conditions and restrictions imposed by the Commission and all the requirements, procedures and matters specified by the Commission.

(3) Any function or power delegated under this section shall be performed and exercised in the name and on behalf of the Commission.

(4) The delegation under this section shall not preclude the Commission from performing or exercising at any time any of the delegated functions and powers.

### Establishment of committees

13. (1) The Commission may establish such committees as it deems necessary or expedient to assist it in the performance of its functions and the exercise of its powers under this Act.

(2) A committee established under subsection (1) —

(a) shall be chaired by any member of the Commission;

<!-- page 10 -->

(b) shall conform to and act in accordance with any direction given to it by the Commission; and

(c) may determine its own procedure.

(3) Members of the committees established under subsection (1) may be appointed from amongst members of the Commission or such other person as the Commission thinks fit.

(4) Except for members of the committees appointed from amongst members of the Commission, any other persons appointed as members of the committees may be paid such allowance as the Prime Minister may determine.

(5) A member of a committee shall hold office for such term as may be specified in his letter of appointment and is eligible for reappointment.

(6) The Commission may revoke the appointment of any member of a committee without assigning any reason therefor.

(7) A member of a committee may, at any time, resign by giving notice in writing to the chairman of the committee.

(8) The Commission may, at any time, discontinue or alter the constitution of a committee.

(9) A committee shall hold its meetings at such times and places as the chairman of the committee may determine.

(10) A committee may invite any person to attend any meeting of the committee for the purpose of advising it on any matter under discussion but that person shall not be entitled to vote at the meeting.

(11) Any person invited under subsection (10) may be paid such allowance as the Prime Minister may determine.

### Responsibility of enforcement agency

14. It shall be the responsibility of every enforcement agency to co- operate with and assist the Commission in the performance by the Commission of its functions and the exercise of its powers under this Act, as and when required by the Commission.

## PART III

## FUNCTIONS AND POWERS OF THE COMMISSION

### Functions and powers of the Commission

15. (1) The functions of the Commission are—

(a) to receive complaints from any person in relation to misconduct of an enforcement officer;

(b) to refer complaints received from any person in relation to misconduct of an enforcement officer to the appropriate Disciplinary Authority or enforcement agency;

(c) to require the Task Force to investigate the complaints of misconduct of an enforcement officer;

(d) to assess the investigation of the Task Force and to determine whether the alleged misconduct of the enforcement officer is of a criminal or disciplinary nature;

(e) to forward investigation paper and other document relating to the misconduct to the Public Prosecutor or Disciplinary Authority for further action;

(f) to receive feedback on the action taken by the Public Prosecutor or Disciplinary Authority and to inform the complainant on the status of the complaint; and

(g) to do such other things as it deems fit to enable it to perform its functions effectively or which are incidental to the performance of its functions under this Act.

(2) The Commission shall have all such powers as may be necessary for, or in connection with, or reasonably incidental to, the performance of its functions under this Act.

## PART IV

## DEALING WITH COMPLAINTS BY THE COMMISSION

### Determination of the nature of complaint

16. (1) The Commission shall consider any complaint lodged by any person, whether written or oral, with the Commission.

<!-- page 12 -->

(2) The Commission shall not consider any complaint relating to any misconduct alleged to be committed by an enforcement officer which —

(a) is the subject matter of any disciplinary proceedings pending before any Disciplinary Authority, including any appeals;

(b) is the subject matter of any proceedings in any court or pending in any court, including any appeals;

(c) has been finally determined by any court; or

(d) is the subject matter of investigation by the enforcement agency.

(3) The Commission shall immediately cease to consider any complaint lodged pursuant to subsection (1) if the complaint becomes the subject matter of any investigation by the enforcement agency under paragraph 2(d).

(4) Where a complaint has been lodged by any person with the Commission in relation to a misconduct of an enforcement officer, the Commission shall, if satisfied that there is no merit in the complaint or the complaint is frivolous or vexatious, dismiss the complaint and notify the complainant in writing of its decision.

(5) If the Commission is satisfied that there is merit in the complaint lodged, the complaint if made orally, shall be reduced into writing and the Commission shall —

(a) if the complaint discloses a misconduct of disciplinary nature and in the opinion of the Commission the complaint may be dealt with by the appropriate Disciplinary Authority, refer the complaint to the appropriate Disciplinary Authority to initiate such disciplinary action in accordance with the provisions of the law applicable to the conduct and discipline of the enforcement officer;

(b) if the complaint discloses a misconduct of disciplinary nature but in the opinion of the Commission the complaint should not be dealt with by the appropriate Disciplinary Authority, refer the complaint to the Task Force for investigation in accordance with the provisions of this Act; or

<!-- page 13 -->

(c) if the complaint discloses a misconduct of criminal nature, refer the complaint to the appropriate enforcement agency to be investigated and dealt with by the enforcement agency in accordance with the powers conferred to it under any written law.

## PART V

## ADMINISTRATION

### Chief Executive Officer

25. (1) The Commission shall appoint a Chief Executive Officer on such terms and conditions as it thinks desirable.

(2) The Chief Executive Officer shall be responsible for the general conduct, administration and management of the function, activities and day to day affairs of the Commission and the carrying out of the decision of the Commission.

<!-- page 18 -->

(3) The Chief Executive Officer shall have general control of the officers and servants of the Commission.

(4) The Chief Executive Officer shall be the Secretary to the Commission.

(5) The Chief Executive Officer shall perform such further duties as the Commission may, from time to time, direct.

(6) In discharging his duties, the Chief Executive Officer shall act under the general authority and direction of the Commission.

(7) If the Chief Executive Officer is temporarily absent from Malaysia or is temporarily incapacitated through illness or for any other reason from the performance of his duties, the Commission may direct any other officer to perform his duties during such temporary absence or incapacity.

### Appointment of the staff

26. (1) The Commission may employ on such terms and conditions as it may think desirable such officers and servants as may be necessary to assist the Commission in the discharge of its functions under this Act.

(2) The Commission may, with the approval of the Prime Minister, make regulations with respect to the terms and conditions of service of its officers and servants.

### Establishment of the Task Force

27. (1) The Commission shall establish a Task Force to assist the Commission in performing its functions under this Act.

(2) The Commission may appoint as members of the Task Force the following persons:

(a) officers of the Anti-Corruption Agency, Central Bank of Malaysia, Securities Commission, National Audit Department, Royal Malaysia Police Force and other officers from the public services of Malaysia on secondment to the Commission;

<!-- page 19 -->

(b) legal officers seconded from the Judicial and Legal Service; and

(c) the officers of the Commission.

(3) The Chief Executive Officer shall be the head of the Task Force. ■ (4) The members of the Task Force shall, subject to the general direction and control of the Commission, perform the functions and duties imposed upon them by the Commission, and may exercise the powers conferred upon them under this Act.

### Power to engage consultants, technical advisors, etc.

28. The Commission may engage retired or former enforcement officers, advocates and solicitors and technical advisors including bankers, accountants and other persons to assist the Commission in the performance of its functions, the exercise of its powers or the discharge of its duties or for the better carrying into effect the purposes of this Act.

## PART VI

## ENFORCEMENT POWERS OF THE COMMISSION AND

## THE TASK FORCE

### Power of investigation

29. (1) The members of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer and the members of the Task Force shall have all the powers necessary to investigate any complaints of misconduct of an enforcement officer referred to the Commission.

(2) For the avoidance of doubt, it is declared that for the purposes of this Act, the members of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer and the members of the Task Force shall have all the powers of a police officer of whatever rank as provided for under the Criminal Procedure Code [Act 593] and such powers shall be in addition to the powers provided for under this Act and not in derogation thereof.

<!-- page 20 -->

### Authority card

30. (1) There shall be issued to each member of the Task Force appointed under section 27 and the Chief Executive Officer, an authority card to be signed by the Chairman of the Commission.

(2) Whenever the Chief Executive Officer or any member of the Task Force exercises any of the powers under this Act, he shall produce to the person against whom the power is being exercised the authority card issued to him under subsection (1)._

### Search and seizure with warrant

31. (1) If it appears to a Magistrate, upon written information on oath from a member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or a member of the Task Force and after such inquiry as the Magistrate considers necessary, that there is reasonable cause to believe that—

(a) any premises has been used for; or

(b) there is in any premises evidence necessary to investigate,

the misconduct, the Magistrate may issue a warrant authorizing the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or any member of the Task Force named therein, at any reasonable time by day or by night and with or without assistance, to enter the premises and if need be by force.

(2) A warrant issued under subsection (1) may authorize the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force to —

(a) search the premises for, and to seize or remove from the premises any conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article that is reasonably believed to furnish evidence of the misconduct;

(b) take samples of any thing found in the premises for the purposes of ascertaining, by testing or otherwise, whether the misconduct has been committed; and

<!-- page 21 -->

(c) make copies of or take extracts from any book, record, document or other article found in the premises.

(3) The member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force entering any premises under this section may take with him such other persons and equipment as may appear to him to be necessary.

(4) The member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force may, in the exercise of his powers under this section, if it is necessary so to do —

(a) break open any outer or inner door of the premises or any fence, enclosure, gate or other obstruction to the premises, in order to effect entry into the premises;

(b) remove by force any obstruction to entry, search, seizure and removal as he is empowered to effect under this section; and

(c) detain any person found in the premises until the search has been completed.

### Search and seizure without warrant

32. Whenever the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force has reasonable cause to believe that any conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book,

<!-- marginal: record, -->

document or other article in respect of which a misconduct has been committed is likely to be found in or on any place, premises, person or conveyance and that by reason of delay in obtaining a warrant under section 31 the object of the search may be frustrated, he may, without warrant, with such assistance and force as is necessary —

(a) enter and search such place or premises;

(b) stop and search such person or conveyance; and

(c) seize any conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article which may be found and may be evidence of the misconduct.

<!-- page 22 -->

### Power to enter premises

33. Notwithstanding sections 31 and 32, the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or any member of the Task Force may at any time enter any premises for the purpose of-

(a) inspecting any conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article as he considers necessary;

(b) verifying the accuracy of records or statement or any information given to the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force; or

(c) collecting samples of any thing relating to the misconduct.

### Seizure of thing, etc.

34. Without prejudice to subsection 31(2) and section 32, any conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article that the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force reasonably suspects has been used in the misconduct may be seized and detained by the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force.

### Power to stop, search and seize conveyances

35. (1) If the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or any member of the Task Force has reasonable cause to suspect that any conveyance is carrying any contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article in respect of which a misconduct has been committed, he may stop and examine the conveyance and may, if on examination he has reasonable cause to believe that such conveyance has been used for the misconduct, seize such conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article found in the conveyance that is reasonably believed to furnish evidence of the misconduct.

<!-- page 23 -->

(2) The person in control or in charge of the conveyance shall, if required to do so by the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force —

(a) stop the conveyance and allow the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force to examine it; and

(b) open all parts of the conveyance for examination and take all measures necessary to enable or facilitate the carrying out of such examination as the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force considers necessary.

(3) Any person who contravenes subsection (2) commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years or to both.

### List of things seized

36. (1) Where any seizure is made under this Act, the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force making the seizure shall prepare a list of every conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article seized and of the place in which it is found and shall sign the list.

(2) The list prepared in accordance with subsection (1) shall be delivered immediately to —

(a) the occupant of the place or premises where the conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article seized is found; and

(b) the person in control or in charge of the conveyance, where the seizure is made under section 35.

(3) Where the seizure is made in or from any place or premises which are unoccupied, the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force shall whenever possible post a list of the things seized conspicuously at the place or premises.

<!-- page 24 -->

(4) Where any conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article is seized otherwise than in or from any place or premises, the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force making the seizure shall give a notice in writing of the seizure and the grounds of the seizure to the owner of the conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article seized by delivering a copy of such notice to the owner in person or by post at his place of business or residence.

(5) A notice under subsection (4) need not be given if the seizure is made in the presence or with the knowledge of the owner.

### Power to require attendance of persons acquainted with case

37. (1) The member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force making an investigation under this Act may, by order in writing, require the attendance before himself of any person who appears to him to be acquainted with the facts and circumstances of the case, and such person shall attend as so required.

(2) If any person refuses to attend as so required, the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force may report such refusal to a Magistrate who shall issue a summons to secure the attendance of such person as may be required by the order made under subsection (1).

### Examination of persons acquainted with case

38. (1) The member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force making an investigation under this Act may examine orally any person supposed to be acquainted with the facts and circumstances of the case.

(2) The person examined under subsection (1) shall be legally bound to answer all questions relating to such case put to him by the member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force, but such person may refuse to answer any question the answer to which would have a tendency to expose him to a criminal charge or penalty or forfeiture.

<!-- page 25 -->

(3) A person making a statement under this section shall be legally bound to state the truth, whether or not such statement is made wholly or partly in answer to the questions.

(4) The member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or member of the Task Force examining a person under subsection (1) shall first inform that person of the provisions of subsections (2) and (3).

(5) A statement made by any person under this section shall, wherever possible, be reduced into writing and signed by the person making it or affixed with his thumbprint, as the case may be-

(a) after it has been read to him in the language in which he made it; and

(b) after he has been given an opportunity to make any correction he may wish.

### Release of seized conveyance, etc.

39. The member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or any member of the Task Force authorized by the Chief Executive Officer may, where he thinks fit, at any time direct that any conveyance, contrivance, equipment, book, record, document or other article seized under this Act be released to the person from whose possession, custody or control it was seized.

## GENERAL

### Funds

40. The Government shall provide the Commission with adequate funds annually to enable the Commission to discharge its functions under this Act.

### Annual report

41. (1) The Statutory Bodies (Accounts and Annual Reports) Act 1980 [Act 240] shall apply to the Commission.

<!-- page 26 -->

(2) The Commission shall, not later than the first meeting of Parliament of the following year, submit an annual report to Parliament of all its activities during the year to which the report relates.

### Obligation of secrecy

42. (1) Except for any of the purposes of this Act or for the purposes of any civil or criminal proceedings under any written law or where otherwise authorized by the Commission —

(a) no member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer, any member of the Task Force and officers and servants of the Commission, and any other person engaged by the Commission pursuant to section 28, whether during or after his tenure of office or employment, shall disclose any information obtained by him in the course of his duties; and

(b) no other person who has by any means access to any information or documents relating to the affairs of the Commission shall disclose such information or document.

(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years or to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to both.

### Accessibility to classified document

43. In discharging their functions under this Act, accessibility to such document relating to official secret by the members of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer, members of the Task Force, officers and servants of the Commission, and any other person engaged by the Commission pursuant to section 28 shall be subject to requirement of the Official Secret Act 1972 [Act 88] or any other written law relating to confidentiality of document or information.

### Protection of informers

44. (1) Except as provided in subsections (2) and (3), no witness in any civil or criminal proceedings shall be obliged or permitted

<!-- page 27 -->

to disclose the name or address of an informer or the substance of the information received from him or to state any matter which might lead to his discovery.

(2) If any book, record or document which is in evidence or liable to inspection in any civil or criminal proceedings contains any entry in which any informer is named or described or which might lead to his discovery, the court shall cause all such passages to be concealed from view or to be obliterated so far only as may be necessary to protect the informer from discovery.

(3) If on the trial for any offence under this Act or any other written law, the court after full inquiry into the case believes that the informer willfully made in his complaint a material statement which he knew or believed to be false or did not believe to be true, or if in any other proceedings the court is of the opinion that justice cannot be fully done between the parties in that proceedings without the discovery of the informer, the court may require the production of the original complaint, if in writing, and permit inquiry and require full disclosure, concerning the informer.

### Protection of officers and servants of the Commission

45. No action, suit, prosecution or proceeding shall be instituted in any court against the Chief Executive Officer, any member of the Task Force or any other officer or servant of the Commission in respect of any act, neglect or default done or committed by him in such capacity provided that he at the time had carried out his functions in good faith.

### Public Authorities Protection Act 1948

46. The Public Authorities Protection Act 1948 [Act 198] shall apply to any action, suit, prosecution or proceedings against the Commission, Chief Executive Officer, any member of the Commission or Task Force or any other officer or servant of the Commission in respect of any act, neglect or default done or committed by him in such capacity.

### Meetings

1. (1) The Commission shall meet at least once a month at such time and place as may be determined by the Chairman.

(3) The common seal shall be kept in the custody of the Chairman or such other person as may be authorized by the Commission, and shall be authenticated by the Chairman or such authorized person or by any officer authorized by either of them in writing; and all deeds, documents and other instruments purporting to be sealed with the said seal, authenticated as aforesaid, shall, until the contrary is proved, be deemed to have been validly executed.

(4) The common seal of the Commission shall be officially and judicially noticed.

4. ### Minutes

5. (1) The Commission shall cause minutes of all its meeting to be maintained and kept in a proper form.

(2) Minutes made of meetings of the Commission shall, if duly signed, be admissible in evidence in all legal proceedings without further proof.

6. No act done or proceeding taken under this Act shall be questioned on the ground of-

(a) a vacancy in the membership of, or a defect in the constitution of, the Commission;

(b) the contravention by any member of the Commission of the provisions of paragraph 4; or

(c) an omission, a defect or an irregularity not affecting the merit of the case.

### Procedure

7. Subject to this Act, the Commission shall determine its own procedure.

### Member of Commission to devote time to business of Commission

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

The Special Complaints Commission Act 2007 ("the Act") as proposed by this Bill contains provisions for the establishment of the Special Complaints Commission, to set out the powers and functions of such Commission, and to provide for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. This Bill is introduced as a result of the recommendations made by the Royal Commission to Enhance the Operation and Management of the Royal Malaysia Police and it is intended to cover not only the police officers but also all enforcement officers at the Federal level.

## PART I

2. Part I of the Act contains preliminary matters.

Clause 1 deals with the short title and seeks to allow the Prime Minister to appoint the date of commencement of the Act. The Act applies only to complaints of misconduct committed by any enforcement officer on or after

<!-- marginal: the -->

commencement of the Act. By subclause (4), the complaint must relate to misconduct committed whilst performing his official duties.

Clause 2 contains the definitions of certain expressions used in the Act.

## PART II

3. Part II contains provisions relating to the Special Complaints Commission ("the Commission").

Clauses 3 and 4 seek to provide for the establishment of the Commission and constitution of the Commission.

Clause 5 contains provision on the tenure of office for certain members of the Commission whilst clause 6 provides for the payment of allowances to members of the Commission.

Clause 7 sets out the circumstances where a member of the Commission may exercise the functions of the Chairman.

Clause 8 pertains to the revocation of appointment of certain members of the Commission and their resignation.

Clause 9 spells out the circumstances in which office of a member of the Commission becomes vacant.

Clause 10 seeks to provide for the protection of any member of the Commission from incurring personal liability for any loss or damage caused by any act or omission in administering the affairs of the Commission, unless the said loss or damage was occasioned by an intentional wrongful act or omission on his part.

<!-- page 31 -->

Clause 11 applies the Schedule to members of the Commission.

Clause 12 seeks to provide for the delegation of the Commission's functions and powers.

Clause 13 seeks to enable the Commission to establish committees to assist in the performance of its functions.

Clause 14 seeks to impose a duty on every enforcement agency to co-operate and assist the Commission in performing its functions and exercising its powers under the Act.

## PART III

4. Part III contains provisions relating to the functions and powers of the Commission.

Clause 15 spells out the functions and powers of the Commission, which includes receiving complaints from any person in relation to misconduct of an enforcement officer and the manner the Commission shall deal with the complaints.

## PART IV

5. Part IV contains provisions on dealing with complaints by the Commission.

Clause 16 contains provisions on how the Commission deals with the complaints it receives. Subclause (/) requires the Commission to consider any complaint lodged with it. Subclauses (2) and (3) describe the nature of complaints not to be considered by the Commission. By subclause (3), the Commission is required to cease considering any complaint if the complaint becomes the subject matter of any investigation. By virtue of subclause (4), the Commission is empowered to dismiss a complaint that is frivolous and vexatious and devoid of merit. Subclause (5) provides that if a complaint bears merit, the Commission shall, if the complaint discloses a misconduct of disciplinary nature, refer the complaint to the appropriate Disciplinary Authority for further action. This is described in paragraph (a). Paragraphs (b) and (c) specify the nature of a misconduct that need to be referred to the Task Force of the Commission and to the appropriate enforcement agency. Subclauses (6) and (7) require the Commission to inform the complainant of such referral, be it to the appropriate Disciplinary Authority or the enforcement agency. It is made clear by these provisions that the Commission is not to indulge in matters that should appropriately be dealt with by a Disciplinary Authority or an enforcement agency so as not to dilute conduct of powers conferred on it by any written law. Subclause (8) provides that upon completion of the investigation referred to in subclause (3), the Public Prosecutor is obliged to communicate his decision on the investigation to the Commission. It also spells out the necessary actions required to be taken by the Commission in relation thereto.

<!-- page 32 -->

Clause 17 seeks to empower the Commission to conduct a preliminary investigation for the purposes of determining the merit of a complaint.

Clauses 18 and 19 impose a duty on the Disciplinary Authority or the enforcement agency to which a complaint has been referred to by the Commission, to submit a monthly report on the progress of the disciplinary proceeding or investigation. The Disciplinary Authority or the enforcement agency may be required to explain why the progress of the disciplinary proceeding or investigation undertaken by it is unsatisfactory. Subclause 18(3) requires the Disciplinary Authority to inform the Commission of the decision made at the end of disciplinary proceedings while subclause 19(3) makes similar requirement to the enforcement agency. Despite the fact that the Commission have no linkage to the procedures undertaken by the Disciplinary Authority and the conduct of criminal investigation by the enforcement agency, clauses 18 and 19 ensure that the Commission still exercises close oversight over the Disciplinary Authority and enforcement agency in handling the complaints from the public. The requirement so imposed also will ensure effective monitoring of complaints by the Commission.

Clause 20 requires the Task Force to submit the investigation papers or any other document to the Commission upon completion of an investigation for the next course of action to be taken pursuant to clause 22.

Clause 21 sets out the circumstances where the Commission may direct the Task Force to conduct further investigation on a complaint.

Clause 22 sets out the procedures to be followed where an investigation discloses that there is a misconduct on the part of an enforcement officer.

Clause 23 requires the Public Prosecutor or the Disciplinary Authority, as the case may be, to communicate its decision to the Commission within thirty days from the date of receipt of the investigation papers.

Clause 24 places a duty on the Commission to inform the complainant of its decision where the complaint is not substantiated following an investigation. The Commission shall also communicate the decision to the complainant after the complaint has been referred to the Disciplinary Authority or the Public Prosecutor.

## PART V

6. Part V of the Bill contains provisions on the administration of the Commission.

Clause 25 contains provisions for appointment of a Chief Executive Officer and sets out his responsibilities.

Clause 26 seeks to empower the Commission to appoint its officers and servants.

Clause 27 deals with the establishment of the Task Force and appointment of its members.

<!-- page 33 -->

Clause 28 seeks to empower the Commission to engage Consultant, technical advisors, etc.

## PART VI

7. Part VI of the Bill contains provisions on the enforcement powers of the Commission and the Task Force.

Clause 29 provides for the power of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer and the members of the Task Force to investigate any complaints of misconduct.

Clause 30 provides that each member of the Task Force and the Chief Executive Officer shall be issued an authority card and shall produce his card when exercising the powers under the proposed Act.

Clauses 31, 32 and 33 deal with provisions relating to the power of search and seizure with warrant, search and seizure without warrant and the power of a member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or a member of the Task Force to enter any premises for the purposes of inspection, verification and collection of samples respectively.

Clause 34 deals with the provision relating to seizure of things, etc.

Clause 35 provides for the power of a member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or a member of the Task Force to stop, search and seize conveyances.

Clause 36 seeks to impose a duty on a member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or a member of the Task Force to prepare a list of things seized.

Clause 37 seeks to allow a member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or a member of the Task Force to order the attendance of persons acquainted with the case.

Clause 38 seeks to empower a member of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer or a member of the Task Force to examine orally any person supposed to be acquainted with the facts and circumstances of the case.

Clause 39 provides for the release of anything seized under the proposed Act.

## PART VII

8. Part VII of the Bill contains general provisions.

Clause 40 deals with the funds of the Commission.

Clause 41 requires the Commission to submit an annual report to Parliament.

<!-- page 34 -->

Clause 42 prohibits the disclosure of information relating to the affairs of the Commission except under certain circumstances.

Clause 43 provides that the Official Secret Act 1972 or any other written law relating to confidentiality of document or information shall apply to every person when assessing to any classified document under the Act.

Clause 44 deals with the provision relating to the protection of informers.

Clause 45 seeks to provide for the protection of members, officers and servants of the Commission.

Clause 46 provides for the application of the Public Authorities Protection Act 1948 to the Commission and its officers or servants.

Clause 47 provides that every member, officer or servant of the Commission shall be deemed to be a public servant within the meaning of the Penal Code while discharging his duties as such.

Clause 48 seeks to provide for the institution and conduct of prosecution.

Clause 49 seeks to empower the Prime Minister to make regulations.

Clause 50 empowers the Prime Minister to make an order for the purpose of removing any difficulties that may arise in connection with the implementation of the Act.
