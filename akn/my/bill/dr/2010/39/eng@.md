## A BILL

intituled

An Act to amend the Merchant Shipping (Oil Pollution) Act

1994. [ ]

ENACTED by the Parliament of Malaysia as follows:

### Short title and commencement

1. (1) This Act may be cited as the Merchant Shipping (Oil Pollution) (Amendment) Act 2010.

(2) This Act comes into operation on a date to be appointed by the Minister by notification in the Gazette.

### Amendment of long title

2. The Merchant Shipping (Oil Pollution) Act 1994 [Act 515], which is referred to as the “principal Act” in this Act, is amended in the long title by inserting after the word “oil” the words “and bunker oil”.

### Amendment of short title

3. The principal Act is amended by substituting for the short title “Merchant Shipping (Oil Pollution) Act 1994” the short title “Merchant Shipping (Liability and Compensation for Oil and Bunker Oil Pollution) Act 1994”.

<!-- page 2 -->

### Reference to the principal Act

4. (1) All references to the Merchant Shipping (Oil Pollution) Act 1994 in any written law or document shall, on the coming into operation of this Act, be construed as references to the Merchant Shipping (Liability and Compensation for Oil and Bunker Oil Pollution) Act 1994.

(2) All references to the Merchant Shipping (Oil Pollution) Act 1994 in any proceedings, whether civil or criminal, or any cause of action commenced, pending or existing before the coming into operation of this Act, shall, on the coming into operation of this Act, continue to apply and not be affected as if it had not been amended by this Act.

### New section 3A

7. The principal Act is amended by inserting after section 3 the following section:

### “Liability for bunker oil pollution

3A. (1) The owner of a ship at the time of an incident, or where the incident consists of a series of occurrences having the same origin, at the time of the first occurrence, shall, except as otherwise provided for by this Act, be liable for any pollution damage caused by the ship as a result of the incident in any area of Malaysia.

(2) Where more than one person is liable in accordance with subsection (1), their liability shall be joint and several.

<!-- page 5 -->

(3) Further, the owner of the ship shall be liable for any pollution damage caused to any area of any other Bunkers Convention country as a result of any incident specified in subsection (1).

(4) Where an incident involving two or more ships occurs and pollution damage results from the incident, the owners of all the ships concerned shall, unless exonerated under section 4, be jointly and severally liable for all such pollution damage which is not reasonably separable.”.

(3) If it is proved that the pollution damage resulted from an act or omission of the owner of the ship, committed with the intent to cause such damage, or recklessly and with the knowledge that such damage would probably result, he shall not be entitled to limit his liability under subsection (2).”.

### “Compulsory insurance against liability for bunker oil

### pollution

11A. (1) Subject to the provisions of this Act relating to Government ships, this section shall apply to any ship having a gross tonnage greater than 1,000 tonnes.

(2) Any such ship shall not enter or leave a port in Malaysia or arrive at or leave a terminal installation area in any area of Malaysia or, if the ship is registered in Malaysia, it shall not enter or leave a port in any other country or a terminal installation in the territorial sea of any other country, unless there is in force a certificate complying with subsection (3) and showing that there is in force in respect of the ship a contract of insurance or other financial security satisfying the requirements of Article 7 of the Bunkers Convention (cover for owner’s liability).

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Merchant Shipping (Oil Pollution) Act 1994 (“Act 515”) to provide for matters relating to the implementation of the International Convention on Civil Liability for Bunker Oil Pollution Damage 2001 (“Bunkers Convention”) in Malaysia.

2. Clause 1 contains the short title and provision on the commencement of the proposed Act.

3. Clause 2 seeks to amend the long title of Act 515 to extend the scope of Act 515 to civil liability for bunker oil pollution.

4. Clause 3 seeks to amend subsection 1(1) of Act 515 in respect of the short title of Act 515. The Act is to be cited as the “Merchant Shipping (Liability and Compensation for Oil and Bunker Oil Pollution) Act 1994” upon the coming into operation of the proposed Act.

5. Clause 4 seeks to provide that any reference to the “Merchant Shipping (Oil Pollution) Act 1994” in any written law or document, shall, on the coming into operation of the proposed Act, be construed as references to the “Merchant Shipping (Liability and Compensation for Oil and Bunker Oil Pollution) Act 1994”. However, any reference to the “Merchant Shipping (Oil Pollution) Act 1994” in any proceedings, whether civil or criminal, or any cause of action commenced, pending or existing before the coming into operation of this proposed Act, shall, on the coming into operation of this proposed Act, continue to apply as if such reference had not been amended by this proposed Act.

6. Clause 5 seeks to amend section 2 of Act 515 to introduce new definitions and amend existing definitions for the purposes of the implementation of the Bunkers Convention.

7. Clause 6 seeks to amend the heading to Part II of Act 515 to extend its scope to provide in respect of civil liability for bunker oil pollution.

8. Clause 7 seeks to introduce a new section 3A that provides for the owner of a ship to be liable for bunker oil pollution from the ship in any area of Malaysia or any area in any other Bunkers Convention country.

9. Clause 8 seeks to amend section 4 of Act 515 to provide for exceptions from liability for bunker oil pollution damage.

<!-- page 18 -->

10. Clause 9 seeks to amend section 5 of Act 515 to specifically provide for circumstances when liability for oil pollution damage under section 3 of Act 515 is excluded.

11. Clause 10 seeks to introduce a new section 5A that establishes the circumstances when liability for bunker oil pollution damage under the new section 3A, as introduced by clause 7, is excluded.

12. Clause 11 seeks to introduce a new section 6A that provides for the liability of an owner for bunker oil damage under the new section 3A, as introduced by clause 7, to be limited in accordance with the Convention on Limitation of Liability for Maritime Claims 1976 as amended by the Protocol of 1996 to amend the Convention on Limitation of Liability for Maritime Claims 1976.

13. Clauses 12, 13, 14 and 15 seek to amend sections 7, 8, 9 and 10 of Act 515, respectively, to extend the application of these sections to liability incurred for bunker oil pollution damage.

14. Clause 16 seeks to state specifically that section 11 of Act 515 provides for compulsory insurance to be maintained by ships in respect of oil pollution.

15. Clause 17 introduces a new section 11A that requires all ships with a gross tonnage of more than 1,000 tons to have compulsory insurance against pollution from bunker oil. The provision includes the requirement for such ships to carry a certificate and empowers the Director of Marine to issue such certificates for Malaysian ships and ships registered in a country which is not a Bunkers Convention country.

16. Clause 18 seeks to amend section 12 of Act 515 to provide that the Director of Marine may issue a certificate to the registered owner, upon being satisfied that there is in respect of the ship a valid contract of insurance or other financial security satisfying the requirements of Article 7 of the Bunkers Convention.

17. Clause 19 seeks to amend section 13 of Act 515 to extend the application of the section to enforce a claim in respect of a liability incurred for bunker oil pollution.

18. Clause 20 seeks to amend section 14 of Act 515 to provide for ships owned by a State and used for commercial purposes to comply with the new section 11A, as introduced by clause 17, in respect of compulsory insurance for bunker oil pollution.

19. Clause 21 seeks to amend section 23 of Act 515 to extend the application of this section to the enforcement of a claim arising from bunker oil pollution damage.

20. Clause 22 seeks to amend section 24 of Act 515 to extend the application of this section to a judgement given by a Court in a Bunkers Convention country to enforce a claim in respect of a liability incurred from bunker oil pollution damage.

<!-- page 19 -->

21. Clause 23 seeks to delete subsection 26(3) of Act 515 as clause 24 provides in respect of the prosecution of any offences under Act 515 or any regulations made thereunder.

22. Clause 24 seeks to introduce a new section 28A which provides that the prosecution of an offence under Act 515 or any regulations made thereunder shall be instituted with the consent of the Public Prosecutor, and provides for persons that may conduct the prosecution.

23. Clause 26 seeks to amend the Second Schedule of Act 515 to update the list of State Parties to the International Convention on Civil Liability for Oil Pollution Damage 1992 and the International Convention on the Establishment of an International Fund for Compensation for Oil Pollution Damage 1992, and adds the list of State Parties to the Bunkers Convention.

24. Other amendments not specifically dealt with in this Statement are minor and consequential in nature.

## FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS

This Bill will not involve the Government in any extra financial expenditure.
