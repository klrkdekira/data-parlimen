## A BILL

intituled

An Act to amend the Private Employment Agencies Act 1981. [ ]

ENACTED by the Parliament of Malaysia as follows:

### Short title and commencement

1. (1) This Act may be cited as the Private Employment Agencies (Amendment) Act 2017.

(2) This Act comes into operation on a date to be appointed by the Minister by notification in the Gazette.

### Amendment of section 2

2. The Private Employment Agencies Act 1981 [Act 246], which is referred to as the “principal Act” in this Act, is amended in section 2—

(a) in the shoulder note, by deleting the words “and exemption”; and

(b) by substituting for subsection (2) the following subsection:

“(2) This Act shall not apply to the Federal Government and State Governments.”.

(b) to regulate all matters relating to the recruiting activity carried on by a private employment agency including issuing a written direction;

### New section 11a

14. The principal Act is amended by inserting after section 11 the following section:

### “Suspension or revocation of licence

11a. (1) The Director General may, by written notice to a private employment agency and without any compensation, suspend or revoke a licence granted under this Act if he is satisfied that the private employment agency—

(a) has contravened or failed to comply with any of the provisions of this Act or any regulations made under this Act;

(b) has failed to comply with any conditions imposed by the Director General;

(c) has been convicted of an offence under this Act or the director of the private employment agency has been detained under any written law in relation to anti-trafficking in persons and forced labour;

(d) has wound up or otherwise dissolved;

(e) has failed to comply with any written directions issued by the Director General under this Act;

(f) has induced the granting of the licence by a false representation of fact;

(g) has used the licence granted under this Act for an unlawful purpose; or

(h) has ceased to carry on recruiting activities.

(2) The Director General shall not suspend or revoke a licence unless he is satisfied that, after giving the private employment agency an opportunity to make any representation in writing as to why the licence should not be suspended or revoked within the period specified in the written notice, the licence should be suspended or revoked.

<!-- page 12 -->

(3) After the expiry of the period specified in the written notice and after considering the representation made under subsection (2), the Director General shall decide whether or not to suspend or revoke the licence.

(4) The Director General shall inform a private employment agency of his decision under subsection (3) by written notice.

(5) The private employment agency shall after receipt of the written notice on the suspension or revocation under subsection (4)—

(a) advertise the notice of cessation of operation in at least one national language newspaper having nationwide circulation;

(b) display the notice of cessation of operation in a conspicuous space at the private employment agency’s premises; and

(c) publish in the private employment agency’s website continuously on the cessation of operation, if any.

(6) Where a licence has been suspended under subsection (3), the Director General shall require the private employment agency to remedy the contravention or to take corrective action within the period as specified by the Director General.

(7) Where the Director General is satisfied that the private employment agency has failed to remedy the contravention or take corrective action as required under subsection (6), the Director General shall revoke the licence of the private employment agency.

(8) A private employment agency may, within twenty-one days from the date of receipt of a revocation notice from the Director General, appeal to the Minister.

(9) Where a licence—

(a) has been revoked, the licence shall have no effect from the date of revocation and the private employment agency shall within fourteen days from the date of revocation of the licence, surrender the licence to the Director General; or

(b) has been suspended, the licence shall have no effect during the period of suspension.”.

13b. (1) A private employment agency shall not assign or transfer any licence granted under this Act to any person unless with the prior written approval of the Director General.

13c. (1) A private employment agency shall not rent out or lease any licence granted under this Act to any person.

13d. (1) A private employment agency which has been granted a licence under section 9a shall apply to the Director General, in such form and manner as determined by the Director General, for an identification document of its employees who carry out recruiting activities.

(3) The Director General may impose any condition for the purpose of granting an identification document to the private employment agency.

(4) Any private employment agency which contravenes subsection (1) or (2) commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding ten thousand ringgit.

(5) Any employee of a private employment agency who fails to produce an identification document when required while carrying out any recruiting activity commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding two thousand ringgit.

(7) Any private employment agency which contravenes subsection (6) commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding ten thousand ringgit.

### Establishment of branch

(2) The conditions as specified under section 9 shall apply for the application of licence under this section.

### Application for changing category of licence

13f. (1) A private employment agency may apply to the Director General to change its category of licence in accordance with the categories of licences as specified in the Second Schedule in such form and manner as determined by the Director General and the application shall be accompanied by the processing fee as specified in the First Schedule.

(2) The Director General may, after considering the application made under subsection (1), approve or refuse the application.

(3) If the Director General approves such application under subsection (2), the Director General shall issue a written notice to the applicant, and within a period of thirty days from the date of a written notice, the applicant shall pay the licence fee as specified in the First Schedule and deposit the money guarantee as specified in the Second Schedule.

### Notice of cessation of operation

13g. (1) A private employment agency which intends to cease operation at any time before the date of the expiry of the licence or does not intend to renew the licence shall, within the period of at least sixty days from the date of cessation of operation—

(a) submit a written notice to the Director General;

(b) advertise the notice of cessation of operation once in at least one daily national language newspaper nationwide;

(c) display the notice of cessation of operation in an appropriate and conspicuous area of the premises of the private employment agency until the last date of the operation; and

### “Registration fee

14a. A private employment agency may impose registration fee as specified in the First Schedule on any job seeker for all categories of employment within or outside Malaysia upon the registration of the job seeker with the private employment agency.

### Placement fee

14b. (1) A private employment agency may impose placement fee as specified in the First Schedule on any job seeker or non-citizen employee upon the acceptance of an offer of employment by them.

(2) The placement fee imposed under subsection (1) may be paid by an employer of the job seeker or non-citizen employee.

(3) If the placement fee is paid in accordance with subsection (2), no placement fee shall be demanded by the private employment agency from the job seeker or non-citizen employee.

### Money guarantee

14c. Upon the grant of a licence by the Director General under section 9a, a private employment agency shall deposit a money guarantee to the Director General in accordance with the category of licence as specified in the Second Schedule.

### Utilization of money guarantee by the Director General

14d. (1) The Director General may, upon giving prior notice to the private employment agency, utilize the money guarantee deposited under this Act for the purpose of ensuring the

<!-- page 19 -->

responsibilities of the private employment agency to the job seeker, non-citizen employee or the employer has been fulfilled.

(2) If the Director General uses the money guarantee under subsection (1), the private employment agency shall issue additional deposit in the form of money guarantee so that the total sum of the money guarantee shall be maintained at all times to be of the value as specified in the Second Schedule after receiving the notice for additional deposit from the Director General.

### Return of money guarantee

14e. (1) The Director General may return a money guarantee to a private employment agency, subject to any deduction, upon a written application from the private employment agency when—

(2) The Director General may impose any terms and conditions on the private employment agency upon receiving the application under subsection (1) for the purpose of approval.

### Forfeiture of money guarantee

(2) Where the private employment agency fails to claim its money guarantee within the period specified in subsection (1), the Director General, shall, by notification in the Gazette, notify the relevant private employment agency that their money guarantee will be forfeited after the expiration of three months from the date of the Gazette.

(3) The money guarantee which is forfeited under subsection (2) shall be paid into the Federal Consolidated Fund.”.

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Private Employment Agencies Act 1981 (“Act 246”). The proposed amendment seeks to make the provisions of the Act more comprehensive by setting out the procedure for application for licence, the conditions and fees imposed and other related matters. The proposed amendment also seeks to enhance the enforcement and offence provisions.

2. Clause 1 contains the short title and provision on the commencement of the proposed Act.

3. Clause 2 seeks to amend section 2 of Act 246 to provide that the Act shall be applicable to all private employment agencies which carry out recruiting activities. However, this Act shall not apply to the Federal Government and State Governments.

4. Clause 3 seeks to amend section 3 of Act 246 to introduce new definitions and to amend certain existing definitions.

5. Clause 4 seeks to amend section 4 of Act 246 to provide for the functions and powers of the Director General.

6. Clause 5 seeks to introduce a new section 4a into Act 246 to provide that the Director General may delegate his functions and power under Act 246 to a labour officer.

7. Clause 7 seeks to amend section 6 Act 246 to provide for the protection of the Government, the Minister, the Director General or any labour officer from any suit and any other legal proceedings subject to the qualifications as stated in the provision.

8. Clause 8 seeks to amend section 7 of Act 246 to require a private employment agency to obtain a valid licence in order to carry out recruiting activities.

9. Clause 9 seeks to amend section 8 of Act 246 to provide more comprehensive procedures for the application of licence under this Act.

10. Clause 10 seeks to amend section 9 of Act 246 to require a private employment agency to comply with the conditions laid down by the Director General under this Act for the purpose of approval of an application for a licence.

11. Clause 11 seeks to introduce new sections 9a and 9b into Act 246. The new sections 9a and 9b empower the Director General to decide whether to grant or refuse a licence under this Act and to impose any conditions on a licence upon granting the licence.

12. Clause 12 seeks to substitute section 10 of Act 246 to provide for the duration of licence.

<!-- page 31 -->

13. Clause 13 seeks to amend section 11 of Act 246 to require a private employment agency to renew its licence in compliance with the conditions specified under this proposed amendment.

14. Clause 14 seeks to introduce a new section 11a into Act 246. The new section 11a seeks to empower the Director General to suspend or revoke the licence of any private employment agency.

15. Clause 15 seeks to amend section 13 of Act 246 to impose obligation on a private employment agency to display its licence in a conspicuous place in the premises and to provide for penalty provision for the contravention of or failure to comply with such obligation.

16. Clause 16 seeks to introduce new sections 13a, 13b, 13c, 13d, 13e, 13F and 13g into Act 246. The new section 13a requires a private employment agency to apply for the replacement of a licence when it is lost, destroyed or damaged whilst the new section 13b seeks to prevent a private employment agency from assigning or transferring ownership of its licence to any person without the consent of the Director General. Further, the new section 13c seeks to prevent a private employment agency from renting out or leasing the licence to any person and the new section 13d seeks to require a private employment agency to apply to the Director General for an identification document for their employees who carry out recruiting activities.

The new sections 13e, 13F and 13g seek to provide for the requirement on a private employment agency to obtain a licence to establish branches, for the application for changing the category of licence by the private employment agency and to require a private employment agency to notify the Director General if the private employment agency intends to cease its operation before the expiry of the licence, respectively.

17. Clause 17 seeks to amend section 14 of Act 246 to prevent a private employment agency from imposing any other fees on a job seeker and non- citizen employee other than as specified in the First Schedule.

18. Clause 18 seeks to introduce new sections 14a, 14b, 14c, 14d, 14e and 14F into Act 246. The new sections 14a and 14b seek to enable a private employment agency to impose registration and placement fees on a job seeker or non-citizen employee as specified in the proposed First Schedule of Act 246, respectively.

The new section 14c seeks to require a private employment agency to furnish a money guarantee as specified in the proposed Second Schedule of this Act. The new section 14d empowers the Director General to utilize the money guarantee deposited by a private employment agency if the agency fails to fulfil its responsibilities towards a job seeker, non-citizen employee and employer.

The new section 14e seeks to provide for the return of the money guarantee to the private employment agency that has ceased operations, has had its licence revoked or the licence has expired. The new section 14F empowers the Director General to forfeit the money guarantee where a private employment agency fails to claim its money guarantee in the event where it has ceased operations, had its licence revoked or the licence has expired.

<!-- page 32 -->

19. Clause 19 seeks to delete sections 15, 16 and 17 of Act 246. Sections 15 and 17 are deleted as the new section 9b now empowers the Director General to impose any condition upon the granting of a licence. Section 16 is deleted because the provision in respect of recruitment of a job seeker outside Malaysia is provided in section 8 and the proposed Second Schedule of this Act.

20. Clause 20 seeks to amend section 18 of Act 246 to provide higher penalties under Act 246.

21. Clause 21 seeks to amend section 19 of Act 46 to impose an obligation on a private employment agency to prepare, maintain and keep the records as determined by the Director General.

22. Clause 22 seeks to introduce a new section 19a into Act 246 to impose an obligation on a private employment agency to notify the Director General of the occurrence of any material change in any information furnished to the Director General.

23. Clause 24 seeks to amend section 21 of Act 246 to empower the Director General and his officers with the powers of a police officer of whatever rank as provided for under the Criminal Procedure Code [Act 593], in relation to enforcement, inspection and investigation.

24. Clauses 25 and 26 seek to delete sections 22, 24 and 25. Section 22 is deleted as a private employment agency is no longer deemed to be a public place. Section 24 is deleted because the forfeiture of money guarantee is now provided in the new section 14F. Section 25 is deleted because revocation of licence is now provided in the new section 11a.

25. Clause 27 seeks to amend section 26 of Act 246 to outline the appeal process by an aggrieved party against a decision made by the Director General under Act 246.

26. Clause 28 seeks to amend section 27 of Act 246 to empower the Minister to prescribe a fine not exceeding two hundred and fifty thousand ringgit for contravention of or failure to comply with any provisions of any regulations made under Act 246.

27. Clause 29 seeks to introduce a new section 27a into Act 246 to empower the Minister to amend the Schedules by an order published in the Gazette.

28. Clause 30 seeks to amend section 28 of Act 246 to provide the offences and penalties under Act 246.

29. Clause 32 seeks to amend section 30 of Act 246 to provide for offences committed by a body corporate.

30. Clause 33 seeks to amend section 31 of Act 246 to provide that proceeding for offences against the Act or against any regulations made shall not be instituted or conducted except after obtaining the consent of the Public Prosecutor.

<!-- page 33 -->

31. Clause 34 seeks to amend section 32 of Act 246 to empower the Minister with the approval of the Public Prosecutor to make regulations prescribing any offence under Act 246 to be compoundable as well as the criteria, method and procedure for compounding such offence. This amendment also seeks to empower the Director General to compound such offences with the consent in writing of the Public Prosecutor.

32. Clause 35 seeks to amend Act 246 by introducing a new First Schedule and Second Schedule. The new First Schedule seeks to provide for the types of fees charged to a private employment agency under Act 246. The new Second Schedule seeks to provide for the categories of the licence, based on the paid–up capital of a private employment agency and the scope of the job placement carried out and the amount of a money guarantee imposed on the private employment agency.

33. Clause 36 deals with savings and transitional provisions.

34. Other amendments not specifically dealt with in this Statement are minor or consequential in nature.

## FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS

This Bill will not involve the Government in any financial expenditure.
