### New sections 12a and 12b

10. The principal Act is amended by inserting after section 12 the following sections:

### “Sole bargaining rights

12a. (1) Where more than one trade unions of workmen have been accorded recognition by the same employer or trade union of employers to represent the same workmen or class of workmen—

(a) the trade unions of workmen concerned may decide among themselves which trade union of workmen shall have the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen and shall notify the Director General; or

(b) if there is no agreement as to which trade union of workmen has the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen, the employer or trade union of employers or any trade union of workmen concerned, may make an application in writing to the Director General to determine which of the trade union of workmen has the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen.

(2) Upon receiving an application in writing under paragraph (1)(b), the Director General may take such steps to determine the application or to make such enquiries by way of secret ballot, to ascertain the highest number of votes indicating the preference for a trade union of workmen to have the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen.

<!-- page 5 -->

(3) Where the result of the secret ballot shows a tie, a further secret ballot shall be carried out by the Director General until the highest number of votes indicating the preference for a trade union of workmen to have the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen is ascertained.

(4) Upon ascertaining the matter under subsection (1), (2) or (3), the Director General shall decide that the trade union of workmen which obtained the highest number of votes shall have the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen.

(5) For the purposes of carrying out his functions under subsection (1), (2) or (3), the Director General—

(a) shall have the power to require the trade union of workmen, the employer or the trade union of employers concerned to furnish such information as he may consider necessary or relevant within the period specified in the requirement; and

(b) may enter any place where any workman is being employed to examine any record or document, or to conduct a secret ballot.

(6) Where the Director General has made a decision under subsection (4), the trade union of workmen which the Director General has decided to have obtained the highest number of votes shall have the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen.

(7) Subject to section 12b, the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen granted pursuant to this section shall cease to exist in the event another trade union of workmen has obtained the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen.

<!-- page 6 -->

### Trade unions obtained sole bargaining rights

12b. Where a trade union of workmen has obtained the sole bargaining rights to represent any workmen or class of workmen whether by a decision of the Director General or otherwise, no other trade unions of workmen shall have the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen unless a period of three years has elapsed after the sole bargaining rights to represent such workmen or class of workmen has been obtained or the trade union of workmen which has obtained the sole bargaining rights is no longer in existence.”.

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Industrial Relations Act 1967 (“Act 177”) to provide among others, for the post of Deputy President of the Industrial Court, to empower the Director General instead of the Minister to refer a complaint to the Industrial Court for hearing, to allow an appeal of any decision of the Industrial Court to be made to the High Court and to provide for the application of Part VI of Act 177 to any statutory authority as determined by the Minister after consultation with the statutory authority.

2. Clause 1 contains the short title of the proposed Act and the power of the Minister to appoint different dates for the coming into operation of different provisions of the proposed Act.

<!-- page 18 -->

3. Clause 2 seeks to define the President of the Industrial Court to include the Deputy President of the Industrial Court.

4. Clause 3 seeks to replace the power of the Minister with the power of the Director General under section 8 of Act 177 to refer a complaint to the Industrial Court for hearing in order to expedite the process of reference of complaints to the Industrial Court.

5. Clause 5 seeks to amend section 9 of Act 177 to expedite dispute resolution processes relating to a dispute as to the capacity of a workman and a dispute in respect of a claim for recognition by a trade union of workmen to represent any workmen or class of workmen, by replacing the power of the Minister with the power of the Director General to resolve the disputes. Clause 5 also removes the discretion of the Director General to refer to the Director General of Trade Unions for him to ascertain the competence of the trade union of workmen concerned to represent any workmen or any class of workmen.

6. Clause 6 seeks to prohibit any workman from going on strike after the Director General has made a decision under section 9 of Act 177 even though the workmen may be dissatisfied with his decision. Previously the decision was made by the Minister.

7. Clause 7 seeks to prohibit any other trade unions of workmen from making a claim for recognition under section 9 of Act 177 until a claim is resolved under paragraph 9(3)(a) or deemed to have been withdrawn under subsection 9(4), or a decision is made by the Director General in respect of a claim for recognition by a trade union in respect of any workmen or a class of workmen under the same section. Previously the decision was made by the Minister.

8. Clause 8 seeks to reduce the time period from three years to one year in which a trade union of workmen is barred from making any claim for recognition in respect of the same workmen or class of workmen in which another trade union of workmen has been accorded recognition. Such recognition which can be accorded by the Minister previously is now accorded by the Director General.

9. Clause 10 seeks to introduce a new section 12A to address issues where there are more than one trade unions of workmen by providing procedures for a trade union of workmen to be selected as the trade union with the sole bargaining rights to represent the same workmen or class of workmen. Clause 10 also seeks to introduce a new section 12b to provide that where a trade union of workmen has obtained the sole bargaining rights to represent any workmen or class of workmen, no other trade unions shall have the same rights for a period of three years or unless the trade union of workmen which has obtained the sole bargaining rights has ceased to exist.

<!-- page 19 -->

10. Clause 11 seeks to amend section 13 of Act 177 to add two procedures in determining recognition to be accorded to a trade union of workmen, that is by the trade unions of workmen or the Director General, pursuant to section 12a. Clause 11 also seeks to bar the employers, trade union of employers and trade union of workmen from extending an invitation to commence collective bargaining until three months before the expiry of the existing collective agreement. This amendment also seeks to enable the trade union of workmen to raise matters of a general character stated in paragraphs 13(3) (a) to (f) of Act 177 in the course of any discussion with an employer or trade union of employers.

11. Clause 12 seeks to amend section 20 of the Act 177 to replace the power of the Minister with the power of the Director General to refer representations of dismissal by a workman to the Industrial Court for an award in order to expedite the process. Clause 12 also seeks to introduce new subparagraphs (6)(a)(iv) and 6(b)(iv) to allow an employer and a workman, additional options during proceedings with the Director General, to be represented by any person, except an advocate and solicitor. Clause 12 further seeks to introduce a new section 20(6A) to enable the next-of-kin of any workman with mental disability to apply to the High Court for an order to appoint a guardian ad-litem for such workman.

12. Clause 13 seeks to introduce a new paragraph 21(1)(aa) into Act 177 for the Yang di-Pertuan Agong to appoint a Deputy President of the Industrial Court.

13. Clauses 14 and 15 seek to amend subsections 22(5) and 23(4) of Act 177 to allow the President of the Industrial Court and the Chairman of the Industrial Court to sit alone when dealing with any complaint of non- compliance of an award for a reference made under subsection 20(3) of Act 177 in order to expedite the process.

14. Clause 16 seeks to enable officers from the ministry charged with the responsibility for human resources with at least fifteen years of experience in labour and industrial relations and who is a qualified person as defined in the Legal Profession Act 1976 [Act 166] to be considered for the appointment as a Chairman under subsection 23(2) of Act 177.

15. Clause 17 seeks to introduce a new subsection 25(3) into Act 177 to restrict the appointment of the Registrar, Deputy Registrars and Assistant Registrars to any person from the Industrial Relations Officers Scheme of Service.

16. Clause 18 seeks to introduce a new proviso under subsection 26(2) into Act 177 to provide that any reference to the Industrial Court without the consent of the parties in respect of a refusal to commence collective bargaining or a deadlock in collective bargaining can only be made subject to certain conditions as listed out in the said subsection.

<!-- page 20 -->

17. Clause 19 seeks to amend section 29 of the Act 177 to empower the Industrial Court to hear and determine the matter before it notwithstanding the date of dismissal as stated in the Director General’s reference is disputed by the parties or is incorrect. Clause 19 also seeks to allow the Industrial Court to continue with the hearing of a case notwithstanding the death of the workman who made the representations.

18. Clause 20 seeks to amend section 30 of Act 177 to enable the Industrial Court to impose interest for awards relating to payment of money as a deterrent measure against the delay in compliance with the awards. Clause 20 also seeks to introduce a new subsection 30(6b) to allow the Industrial Court to award backwages or compensation in lieu of reinstatement or both to the next-of-kin of the deceased workman.

19. Clause 21 seeks to introduce a new paragraph 32(1)(e) into Act 177 to provide that any award made by the Industrial Court is binding on the next-of-kin of the deceased workman in relation to a reference under subsection 20(3) of Act 177.

20. Clause 22 seeks to delete section 33a of the Act 177 in respect of a reference to the High Court on a question of law by the Industrial Court.

21. Clause 24 seeks to introduce a new section 33c into Act 177. The new section 33c enables an aggrieved party who is not satisfied with an award of the Industrial Court to appeal to the High Court.

22. Clause 25 seeks to amend subsection 40(3) of Act 177 to remove the imprisonment sentence and to increase the fine from one thousand ringgit to five thousand ringgit.

23. Clause 26 seeks to introduce a new subsection 44a into Act 177 to enable the Minister to stop a strike or lock-out if it endangers the life, personal safety or health of the whole or part of the population.

24. Clause 27 seeks to amend section 46 of Act 177 to remove the imprisonment sentence and to increase the fines in subsection 46(1) and (2) of Act 177 from one thousand ringgit to five thousand ringgit.

25. Clause 28 seeks to amend section 47 of Act 177 to increase the fine from one thousand ringgit to five thousand ringgit.

26. Clause 29 seeks to amend section 48 of Act 177 to increase the fine from five hundred ringgit to five thousand ringgit.

27. Cause 30 seeks to introduce a new subsection 52(3) into Act 177 to extend the application of Part VI of Act 177 to any statutory authority as precisbed by the Minister by order in the Gazette.

<!-- page 21 -->

28. Clause 31 seeks to amend section 56 of Act 177 to increase the fine from two thousand ringgit to fifty thousand ringgit to encourage compliance with an order of the Industrial Court under subsection 56(2) of Act 177. Clause 31 also introduces a new subsection 56(3a) to enable the Industrial Court to order a person convicted of an offence under subsection 56(3) of Act 177 to make payment due to the workman by virtue of an order made under section 30 of Act 177.

29. Clause 32 seeks to amend section 60 of Act 177 to increase the fine from five thousand ringgit to fifty thousand ringgit to encourage compliance with Act 177 and its regulations.

30. Clause 33 seeks to amend section 62 of Act 177 to empower the Minister to make regulations to provide for the procedures relating to the process of determining the sole bargaining rights.

31. Clause 35 seeks to provide for saving and transitional provisions.

32. Other amendments not specifically dealt with in this Statement are minor or consequential in nature.

## FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS

This Bill will involve the Government in extra financial expenditure the amount of which cannot at present be ascertained.
