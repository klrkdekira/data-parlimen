### “Paternity leave

60 fa. (1) Subject to subsection (3), a married male employee shall be entitled to a paid paternity leave at his ordinary rate of pay for a period of three consecutive days in respect of each confinement.

(2) The paternity leave under subsection (1) shall be restricted to five confinements irrespective of the number of spouses.

### Substitution of section 60k

24. The principal Act is amended by substituting for section 60k the following section:

### “Employment of foreign employee

60k. (1) No employer shall employ a foreign employee unless prior approval has been obtained from the Director General.

(2) An application for the approval under subsection (1) shall be made in the form and manner as may be determined by the Director General.

(3) Upon approval of the Director General under this section, an employer shall, within fourteen days from the date of the employment of a foreign employee, furnish the Director General with the particulars relating to the foreign employee in such manner as the Director General may direct.

(4) The Director General may, subject to any written law, approve an application under this section if the employer complies with the following conditions:

(a) the employer satisfies the Director General that on the date on which he makes the application—

(i) he has no outstanding matter relating to any decision, order or directive issued under this Act; or

(ii) he has no outstanding matter or case relating to any conviction for any offence under this Act, the Employees’ Social Security Act 1969 [Act 4], the Employees’ Minimum Standards of Housing, Accommodations and Amenities Act 1990 [Act 446] or the National Wages Consultative Council Act 2011 [Act 732]; or

(b) the employer has not been convicted of any offence under any written law in relation to anti-trafficking in persons and forced labour.

### “Presumption as to who is an employee and employer

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Employment Act 1955 (“Act 265”) to comply with the international standards and practices as required by the Trans-Pacific Partnership Agreement, the Malaysia-United States Labour Consistency Plan and the International Labour Organization. The purpose of this amendment among others, is to provide for the protection against discrimination and forced labour, and to provide for maternity benefits.

2. Clause 1 contains the short title and the provision on the commencement of the proposed Act.

3. Clause 2 seeks to amend subsection 2(1) of Act 265 to introduce new definition and to amend certain existing definitions.

4. Clause 3 seeks to amend section 4 of Act 265 to extend the duration for an appeal to be made to the Director General.

5. Clause 4 seeks to introduce a new section 18a into Act 265 to provide for the calculation of wages where an employee has not completed a whole month of service.

6. Clause 5 seeks to amend subsection 22(2) of Act 265 to extend the definition of “immediate family members” to spouse.

7. Clause 6 seeks to amend section 25 of Act 265 to be consistent with the Financial Services Act 2013 [Act 758], the Islamic Financial Services Act 2013 [Act 759] and the Development Financial Institutions Act 2002 [Act 618]. Under the new subsection 25(4), the Minister may, by order, recognize an approved issuer of designated payment instrument for the purpose of payment of wages.

<!-- page 21 -->

8. Clause 7 seeks to amend section 25a of Act 265 to insert new requirements for the payment of wages other than through financial institution.

9. Clause 9 seeks to amend section 33a of Act 265 to insert additional requirements for a contractor for labour and to increase the amount of penalty imposed for failure to comply with the requirements under section 33a of Act 265 from ten thousand ringgit to fifty thousand ringgit.

10. Clause 10 seeks to delete Part VIII of Act 265 to comply with the requirements of the International Labour Organization.

11. Clause 12 seeks to amend section 37 of Act 265 to increase the period of maternity leave from sixty to ninety days and other matters relating to it.

12. Clause 13 seeks to introduce a new section 41a into Act 265 to restrict the termination of pregnant female employee.

13. Clauses 15 and 29 seek to delete sections 44a, 69b, 69c, 69d and 69e of Act 265 as a consequence of the extension of the scope of Act 265. This would allow an employee to bring any dispute before the Director General irrespective of the amount of wages he receives which under the current provisions is capped at five thousand ringgit.

14. Clauses 16 and 17 seek to amend the heading of Part XI and section 57 of Act 265 to substitute for the word “servant” the word “employee”.

15. Clauses 18 and 19 seek to amend sections 57a and 57b of Act 265 to substitute for the word “servant” the word “employee” and to increase the amount of penalty imposed from ten thousand ringgit to fifty thousand ringgit for failure to inform the Director General on the employment and termination of a foreign employee.

16. Clauses 20 and 21 seek to amend sections 60a and 60c of Act 265 as a consequence of the reduction of hours of work in one week from forty-eight to forty-five hours.

17. Clause 22 seeks to amend section 60f of Act 265 to provide for matters relating to the entitlement of paid sick leave.

18. Clause 23 seeks to introduce a new section 60fa into Act 265 to provide for matters relating to paternity leave for a married male employee.

19. Clause 24 seeks to substitute the existing section 60k of Act 265 to introduce new requirements for the employment of a foreign employee.

20. Clause 25 seeks to introduce a new section 60ka into Act 265 to provide for matters relating to the termination of employment of a foreign employee.

21. Clause 27 seeks to introduce a new Part XIIc into Act 265 relating to flexible working arrangement for an employee.

<!-- page 22 -->

22. Clause 28 seeks to amend section 69 of Act 265 to increase the amount of penalty imposed under that section for failure to comply with any order or decision of the Director General upon an inquiry under that section.

23. Clause 30 seeks to introduce a new section 69f into Act 265 to empower the Director General to inquire into dispute relating to the discrimination in employment and make any order or decision.

24. Clause 34 seeks to amend section 81f of Act 265 to increase the amount of penalty imposed from ten thousand ringgit to fifty thousand ringgit on employers for the offence of failure to inquire into complaints of sexual harassment.

25. Clause 36 seeks to introduce a new section 81h into Act 265 relating to the duty of employers to exhibit a notice to raise awareness on sexual harassment.

26. Clause 38 seeks to amend section 84 of Act 265 to empower the court of a First Class Magistrate to try any offence and to impose the full punishment under Act 265.

27. Clause 40 seeks to introduce a new section 87a into Act 265 to empower the court to order an employer to pay any payment due to an employee.

28. Clause 41 seeks to introduce a new section 90b into Act 265 to provide for the offence of forced labour.

29. Clause 43 seeks to amend section 99a of Act 265 to increase the amount of penalty imposed from ten thousand ringgit to fifty thousand ringgit for any offence under Act 265 and regulations made under the Act for which no penalty is prescribed.

30. Clause 44 seeks to introduce a new section 101c into Act 265 on the presumption as to who is employee and employer of such employee in the absence of a contract of service.

31. Clause 45 seeks to amend section 102 of Act 265 to provide for additional matters for which the Minister may make regulations under Act 265.

32. Clause 46 deals with the saving and transitional provisions.

33. Other amendments not specifically dealt with in this Statement are minor or consequential in nature.

## FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS

This Bill will involve the Government in extra financial expenditure the amount of which cannot at present be ascertained.
