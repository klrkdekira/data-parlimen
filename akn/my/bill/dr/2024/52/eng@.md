## BAR COUNCIL LEGAL AID CENTRE

“legal aid” means the legal services provided by the Centre to an aided person, including representing the aided person in any proceedings;

“means test” means a test, conducted by the Bar Council, to determine the eligibility of a person for legal aid.

### Bar Council Legal Aid Centre

76b. (1) The Bar Council shall administer a centre known as the “Bar Council Legal Aid Centre” with one or more branches in each State for the purpose of carrying out the functions under section 76c.

(2) The centre shall be responsible to the Bar Council, and shall—

(a) furnish the Bar Council with reports on its activities, its annual accounts audited by the Bar Council’s auditors and any other information as the Bar Council may require; and

(b) cause its annual audited report to be presented at the annual general meeting of the Malaysian Bar.

### Functions of the Centre

76c. (1) The Centre shall have the following functions:

(a) to provide legal aid to aided persons;

(b) to promote legal literacy in a manner determined and approved by the Bar Council;

(c) to assist the public in matters relating to justice;

(d) to engage the service of members of the Malaysian Bar in the performance of its functions;

(f) to maintain a client account for any aided person, in accordance with any rules made pursuant to section 76m;

(g) to carry on any activity that is relevant to, or in connection with, the performance of its functions;

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill seeks to amend the Legal Profession Act 1976 (“Act 166”). The purpose of these amendments among others, to enhance the legal profession by allowing legal practice by way of limited liability law partnership.

2. Clause 1 contains the short title and the power of the Minister to appoint the commencement date of the proposed Act.

3. Clause 2 seeks to amend section 3 of Act 166 to introduce the new definitions of “limited liability law partnership” and “limited liability partnership” into Act 166.

4. Clause 3 seeks to introduce a new section 9a into Act 166 to empower the Auditor General to audit the accounts of the Board annually.

5. Clause 4 seeks to amend subsection 11(1) of Act 166 to introduce a new paragraph 11(1)(e) into Act 166 to provide that, in addition to the other requirements in subsection 11(1) of Act 166 for the admission as an advocate and solicitor, a qualified person may be admitted as an advocate and solicitor if he has obtained an order under subsection 36(2) of Act 166.

6. Clause 5 seeks to amend subsection 15(3) of Act 166 to introduce a new paragraph 15(3)(h) to provide that every petitioner shall, not less than fourteen days before his petition is to be heard or such shorter period as the Court may allow, file an affidavit exhibiting, in addition to the other documents specified in subsection 15(3) of Act 166, a true copy of the order obtained under subsection 36(2) of Act 166.

7. Clause 6 seeks to amend subsection 30(1) of Act 166 in order not to prohibit an advocate and solicitor from applying for a practising certificate if he is practising or intends to practice in a limited liability law partnership in Malaysia or if he is or is about to be, employed full time in his practice in Malaysia by a limited liability law partnership.

8. Clause 7 seeks to amend section 36 of Act 166 to allow the Master of a pupil to make an application to a Judge for an order that the Master’s pupil be permitted to appear in court on behalf of the Master or on behalf of the law firm or the limited liability law partnership where the Master is practising within thirty days from the commencement of the pupillage of the Master’s pupil. This Clause also seeks to prohibit any application under subsection 36(2) of Act 166 be made after the expiry of thirty days from the commencement of the pupillage of the Master’s pupil unless the application is made with the leave of court.

9. Clause 8 seeks to amend section 40a of Act 166 to introduce a new definition of “Malaysian law firm”.

10. Clause 9 seeks to amend subsection 40f(1) of Act 166 to allow a limited liability law partnership formed pursuant to an arrangement between a foreign law firm and Malaysian law firm to apply to the Bar Council for an international partnership licence.

12. Clause 11 seeks to introduce a new Part IVb into Act 166 to provide for matters relating to limited liability law partnerships.

The proposed new section 40P contains the definition of certain words and expressions used in Part IVb.

The proposed new section 40q provides that the approval to form a limited liability law partnership or to convert an existing firm into a limited liability law partnership for the purpose of carrying on the practice of advocates and solicitors shall be obtained by an advocate and solicitor from the Bar Council.

The proposed new section 40r empowers the Bar Council to approve the formation of a limited liability law partnership or the conversion of an existing firm into a limited liability law partnership. However, a limited liability law partnership approved by the Bar Council shall not be recognized to be a limited liability law partnership under Act 166 until it is registered under the Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2012 [Act 743] and its name is entered into the register under the proposed new subsection 40z(2).

The proposed new section 40s empowers the Bar Council to approve the proposed name of a limited liability law partnership subject to certain conditions, and such name shall not be changed or altered without the prior approval in writing from the Bar Council.

The proposed new section 40t provides that a limited liability law partnership to do anything that an advocate and solicitor may do by law and shall do all things that an advocate and solicitor is required to do by law, but shall not include anything that may only be done by an advocate and solicitor as a natural person.

The proposed new section 40u deals with the effects of the conversion of a firm into a limited liability law partnership to the clients, assets and property, documents, lien or security, etc., of the firm.

The proposed new section 40v provides that a limited liability law partnership shall have the rights and shall be subject to the fiduciary, confidential and ethical requirements with respect to each client of the limited liability law partnership same as the rights and requirements that exist at law with respect to an advocate and solicitor and his client.

The proposed new section 40w provides that a partner, consultant or employee of a limited liability law partnership who is an advocate and solicitor providing services which only can be provided by an advocate and solicitor under Act 166 shall be subject to the same standards of conduct and competence in respect of such services as if he was personally providing such services as an advocate and solicitor in a firm.

The proposed new section 40x prohibits a limited liability law partnership from carrying on the practice of an advocate and solicitor if at any time any partner of the limited liability law partnership is not a practising advocate and solicitor.

The proposed new section 40y allows the High Court, upon application made by the Bar Council, to order the winding up of a limited liability law partnership under the Limited Liability Partnership Act 2012 on certain grounds. However, the winding up of a limited liability law partnership shall not prejudice any right to institute disciplinary proceedings against an advocate and solicitor practising under the limited liability law partnership or any right of the Bar Council or State Bar Committee to exercise any rights or powers under Act 166.

The proposed new section 40z requires the Bar Council to keep and maintain a register of the limited liability law partnerships approved under the proposed new section 40r in such form and manner as the Bar Council thinks fit and to allow any person to inspect such register.

The proposed new section 40za deals with the right of an aggrieved person to appeal to the High Court against the decision of the Bar Council under the proposed new section 40r or 40s.

The proposed new section 40zb provides that the new Part IVb and any rules made under the proposed new section 40zd shall prevail over any inconsistent provision of any limited liability partnership agreement.

The proposed new section 40zc provides that the new Part IVb and any rules made under the proposed new section 40zd shall be read together with the Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2012 and in the case of conflict or inconsistency between the new Part IVb and the Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2012, the provisions of the new Part IVb shall prevail.

The proposed new section 40zd empowers the Bar Council, with the approval of the Attorney General, to make rules in respect of limited liability law partnership for the purposes of the new Part IVb.

Clause 50 seeks to amend section 141 of Act 166 to extend the application of section 141 of Act 166 to a limited liability law partnership to provide, amongst others, that no bank shall incur any liability or be under any obligation to make any inquiry, or be deemed to have any knowledge of any right of any person to any money paid or credited to the account of a limited liability law partnership.

48. Clause 51 seeks to rename the existing Schedule to Act 166 as the First Schedule and to extend the application of the First Schedule to a limited liability law partnership with regard to the control of property in certain cases.

49. Clause 52 seeks to introduce a new Second Schedule into Act 166 which contains forms in relation to the proposed new sections 76h, 76i and 76k.

50. Other amendments not specifically dealt with in this Explanatory Statement are minor or consequential in nature.

## FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS

This Bill will not involve the Government in any extra financial expenditure.
