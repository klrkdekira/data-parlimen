## A BILL

intituled

An Act to prevent and combat cybercrimes which consists of offences provided for under Parts III to VI of this Act and offences provided for under any other written law committed by means of a computer system, and to provide for related matters. [ ]

ENACTED by the Parliament of Malaysia as follows:

## Part I

## PRELIMINARY

### Short title and commencement

1. (1) This Act may be cited as the Cybercrimes Act 2026.

(2) This Act comes into operation on a date to be appointed by the Minister by notification in the Gazette.

### Extra-territorial application

2. (1) This Act shall, in relation to any person, whatever his nationality or citizenship, have effect outside as well as within Malaysia, and where an offence under this Act is committed by any person in any place outside Malaysia, he may be dealt with in respect of such offence as if the offence was committed at any place within Malaysia.

<!-- page 6 -->

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), this Act shall apply if for the offence in question, the computer system, program or computer data was in Malaysia or connected to or sent to or used by or with a computer system, program or computer data in Malaysia at the material time or the person affected by the commission of the offence is a Malaysian citizen.

### Interpretation

3. In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

“technical means” includes the use of any device, equipment, software, program, computer code, network or other technological means;

“content data” means any computer data, other than subscriber information or traffic data, relating to the substance of the data transferred by a computer system, including images, text messages, voice messages, audio recordings and video recordings;

“computer data” means any representation of facts, information or concepts in a form suitable for processing in a computer system which includes a program suitable to cause a computer system to perform a function, including content data, traffic data and subscriber information;

“traffic data” means any computer data relating to a communication by means of a computer system, generated by a computer system that forms a part in the chain of communication, indicating the communication’s origin, destination, route, time, date, size, duration or type of underlying service;

“property” means an asset of every kind, whether corporeal or incorporeal, movable or immovable property, tangible or intangible, including virtual asset, digital currency and legal documents or instruments evidencing title to, or interest in, such asset;

“Committee” means the Committee on Combating Cybercrimes established under section 4;

“Chief Executive” means the Chief Executive of the National Cyber Security Agency;

<!-- page 7 -->

“communications” means any communication, whether between persons and persons, things and things, or persons and things, in the form of sound, data, text, visual images, signals or any other form or any combination of those forms;

“subscriber information” means any information held by a service provider, relating to subscribers of its services, other than traffic data or content data, and which can be established by—

(a) the type of communication service used by the subscriber and the related technical provisions and period of service;

(b) the subscriber’s identity, address, telephone or other access number, billing or payment information, available on the basis of the service agreement or arrangement; or

(c) any other information on the site of the installation of communication equipment, available on the basis of the service agreement or arrangement;

“Minister” means the Minister charged with the responsibility for matters relating to cybercrimes;

“authorized officer” means any police officer, or any other public officer or officer of the Commission authorized in writing by the Minister under section 26;

“officer of the Commission” means an officer of the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission;

“service provider” means a person, whether licensed under the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 [Act 588] or not, who—

(a) provides to users of its services the ability to communicate by means of a computer system;

(b) processes or stores computer data on behalf of a communications service or users of the communications service; or

(c) provides an information and communication service, including telecommunications;

<!-- page 8 -->

“program” means a set of instructions or statements that when executed in a computer system causes the computer system to perform a function;

“computer system” means any device or a group of interconnected or related devices, one or more of which, pursuant to a program, gathers, stores and performs automatic processing of computer data, including a computer;

“Commission” means the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission established under section 4 of the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission Act 1998 [Act 589].

## COMMITTEE ON COMBATING CYBERCRIMES

(2) The Committee shall consist of the following members:

(a) the Chief Secretary to the Government who shall be the Chairman;

(b) the Director General of National Security who shall be the Deputy Chairman;

(c) the Secretary General of Treasury or his representative;

(d) the Attorney General or his representative;

(e) the Inspector-General of Police or his representative;

(f) the Secretary General of the Ministry charged with the responsibility for home affairs or his representative;

(g) the Secretary General of the Ministry charged with the responsibility for communications or his representative;

<!-- page 9 -->

(h) the Secretary General of the Ministry charged with the responsibility for foreign affairs or his representative;

(i) the Secretary General of the Ministry charged with the responsibility for domestic trade and cost of living or his representative;

(j) the Secretary General of the Ministry charged with the responsibility for digital related matters or his representative;

(k) the Personal Data Protection Commissioner or his representative;

(l) the Director General of the Legal Affairs Division of the Prime Minister’s Department or his representative;

(m) the Governor of the Central Bank of Malaysia or his representative;

(n) the Director General of the National Anti-Financial Crime Centre or his representative;

(o) the Chairman of the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission or his representative;

(p) the Chairman of the Securities Commission or his representative;

(q) the Chief Executive Officer of the Companies Commission of Malaysia or his representative; and

(r) not more than two other persons who shall be appointed by the Chairman from among persons of standing, experience, knowledge and expertise in matters relating to cybercrimes.

(3) The Chief Executive shall be the secretary to the Committee.

<!-- page 10 -->

### Functions of Committee

5. (1) The Committee shall have the following functions:

(a) to plan, formulate and decide on approaches and strategies in preventing and combating cybercrimes;

(b) to advise and make recommendations to the Government on policies and strategic measures to prevent and combat cybercrimes;

(c) to coordinate initiatives to prevent and combat cybercrimes in an integrated manner among the enforcement agencies;

(d) to assess and oversee the effectiveness of existing actions and mechanisms in preventing and combating cybercrimes;

(e) to identify gaps and recommend improvements in the legal and enforcement framework relating to cybercrimes; and

(f) to do such other things arising out of or consequential to the functions of the Committee under this Act consistent with the purposes of this Act.

(2) The Committee shall have all such powers as may be necessary for, or in connection with, or reasonably incidental to, the performance of its functions under this Act.

### Meetings of Committee

6. (1) The Committee shall convene its meeting as often as the Chairman may determine and the meeting shall be held at the time and place as determined by the Chairman.

(2) The Chairman shall preside at all meetings of the Committee.

(3) If the Chairman is absent from any meeting of the Committee, he may direct the Deputy Chairman to replace him as the chairman of the meeting.

(4) The quorum of the Committee shall be five.

<!-- page 11 -->

(5) The Chairman may authorize the use of a live video link, live television link or any other electronic means of communication for the purposes of any meeting of the Committee.

(6) The Committee may determine its own procedure.

### Committee may invite others to attend meetings

7. The Committee may invite any person to attend meetings of the Committee to advise the Committee on any matter under discussion.

### Committee may establish subcommittees

8. (1) The Committee may establish any subcommittee as the Committee considers necessary or expedient to assist the Committee in the performance of its functions.

(2) The Committee may appoint any person to be a member of any subcommittee established under subsection (1).

(3) The Committee may appoint any of its members or any other person to be the chairman of the subcommittee established under subsection (1).

(4) The subcommittee shall be subject to and act in accordance with any directions given by the Committee.

(5) The subcommittee shall meet as often as may be necessary at the time and place as the chairman of the subcommittee may determine.

(6) The subcommittee may invite any person to attend meetings of the subcommittee to advise the subcommittee on any matter under discussion.

(7) The chairman of the subcommittee may authorize the use of a live video link, live television link or any other electronic means of communication for the purposes of any meeting of the subcommittee.

## Part III

## OFFENCES RELATING TO CONFIDENTIALITY, INTEGRITY AND

## AVAILABILITY OF COMPUTER SYSTEM AND COMPUTER DATA

### Access to computer system

9. For the purposes of this Part—

(a) a person secures access to the whole or any part of a computer system if the person causes a computer system to perform any function, including by communicating with, instructing, storing data on, retrieving data from, altering or erasing any program or data in, or otherwise making use of any resource of, the computer system; and

(b) a person secures access of any kind to the whole or any part of a computer system without authority if in securing access—

(i) the person is not entitled to control access of the kind in question to the computer system; and

(ii) the person does not have the consent or exceeds any right or consent given, to access by him of the kind in question to the computer system from any person who is so entitled.

### Access to computer system without authority

10. (1) Any person who, intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose, secures access to the whole or any part of a computer system commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years or to both.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), it is immaterial whether the access to the computer system is directed at any particular program or computer data in the computer system.

<!-- page 13 -->

### Access to computer system without authority with intention

### to commit or facilitate commission of further offence

11. (1) Any person who commits an offence under section 10 with the intention—

(a) to commit a further offence involving fraud or dishonesty or which causes injury as defined in the Penal Code [Act 574]; or

(b) to facilitate the commission of such further offence whether by himself or by any other person,

commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), it is immaterial whether such further offence is to be committed at the same time when the access is secured without authority or on any future occasion.

### Interception without authority

12. (1) Any person who, intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose, intercepts by technical means any non-public transmission of computer data to, from or within a computer system, including any electromagnetic emissions from a computer system carrying computer data, commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), it is immaterial whether the interception is directed at any particular non-public transmission of computer data.

<!-- page 14 -->

### Computer data interference

13. Any person who, intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose—

(a) damages, deletes, deteriorates, alters, suppresses or modifies computer data;

(b) renders computer data meaningless, useless or ineffective;

(c) obstructs, interrupts or interferes with the lawful use of computer data; or

(d) denies access to computer data to any person entitled to it,

commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years or to both.

### Computer system interference

14. Any person who, intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose, inputs, transmits, damages, deletes, deteriorates, alters or suppresses computer data so as to seriously hinder or interfere with the functioning of a computer system or lawful use or operation of a computer system commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

### Misuse of device

15. (1) Any person who, intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose, obtains, procures for use, produces, sells, imports or exports, distributes, discloses or otherwise makes available—

(a) a device including a program, that is designed or adapted primarily for the purpose of committing any offence under this Act; or

<!-- page 15 -->

(b) a password, access credentials, electronic signature or similar computer data by which the whole or any part of a computer system or computer data is capable of being accessed,

with the intention that a device including a program, password, access credentials, electronic signature or similar computer data be used by himself or any other person for the purpose of committing an offence under this Act, commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

(2) Any person who has in his possession or control any device referred to in paragraph (1)(a) or any password, access credentials, electronic signature or computer data referred to in paragraph (1)(b) with the intention that the device, password, access credentials, electronic signature or computer data be used by himself or by any other person for the purpose of committing any offence under this Act commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to both.

## Part IV

## OFFENCES RELATING TO COMPUTER-RELATED FORGERY AND

## COMPUTER-RELATED FRAUD

### Computer-related forgery

16. (1) Any person who, intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose, inputs, alters, deletes or suppresses computer data resulting in inauthentic computer data with the intention that such computer data be considered or acted upon for any legal purpose as if the computer data were authentic, whether or not such computer data is intended to deceive any particular person, commits an offence and shall, on conviction—

(a) where valuable security is involved, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both; or

<!-- page 16 -->

(b) in any other case, be liable to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to both.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1)—

(a) “valuable security” means a document which is, or purports to be, a document that creates, extends, transfers, restricts, extinguishes or releases any legal right, or by which a person acknowledges that he lies under legal liability or does not have a certain legal right; and

(b) it is immaterial whether the computer data in question is directly readable or intelligible.

### Computer-related fraud

17. Any person who, intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose—

(a) inputs, alters, deletes or suppresses any computer data;

(b) interferes with the functioning of a computer system; or

(c) causes deception as to factual circumstances by means of a computer system that causes a person to do or omit to do anything which that person would not otherwise do or omit to do,

so as to cause loss of property to another person with the fraudulent or dishonest intention to procure without authority economic benefit for himself or for another person commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one million ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten years or to both.

## Part V

## OFFENCES RELATING TO NATIONAL DIGITAL IDENTITY SERVICE

### Interpretation

18. For the purposes of this Part—

(a) “credential”, in relation to a user includes—

(i) the user’s national digital identity service password;

(ii) the user’s biometric identifier which includes any personal data resulting from technical processing relating to the physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics of a person captured electronically; and

(iii) the user’s digital certificate;

(b) “user” means a person who is registered with the national digital identity service; and

(c) “national digital identity service” means the electronic service owned by the Federal Government by which a user’s identity is verified and authenticated.

### Disclosure of password or provision of other means of securing

### access in identity of user

19. (1) Any user who—

(a) discloses his national digital identity service password to any person; or

(b) provides to any person any other means of securing access in the identity of the user to any program or computer data in any computer system by means of the national digital identity service,

<!-- page 18 -->

knowing or having reasonable grounds to believe that the purpose of the disclosure or provision is for any person to commit or to facilitate the commission by any person of, any offence under any written law, commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years or to both.

(2) In any proceedings for an offence under subsection (1), if it is proven that the user discloses his national digital identity service password or provides any other means of securing access in the identity of the user to any person—

(a) for any gain, whether or not the gain is a wrongful gain, realized, or for himself or for another person; or

(b) knowing that the disclosure or provision is likely to cause wrongful loss to any person,

it shall be presumed until the contrary is proved that the user knew or had reasonable grounds to believe that the disclosure or provision was for the purpose of committing or facilitating the commission of, an offence under any written law.

### Obtaining, etc., credential

20. (1) Any person who obtains, retains, supplies, offers to supply, transmits or otherwise makes available any credential of any user—

(a) for the purpose of committing or facilitating the commission of, any offence under any written law; or

(b) knowing or having reason to believe that the credential will be used, or is likely to be used, for the purpose of committing, or facilitating the commission of, any offence under any written law,

commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years or to both, and for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to both.

<!-- page 19 -->

(2) It shall not be an offence under subsection (1) for a person to transmit or otherwise make available credentials of any user by reason only that the person, in the ordinary course of providing such facilities or services, provides or operates facilities for network access, or provides services relating to, or connections for, the transmission, routing or storage of data.

## Part VI

## OTHER OFFENCES

### Wrongful communication

21. Any person who communicates directly or indirectly a number, code, password or other means of access to a computer system to any person other than a person to whom he is duly authorized to communicate such number, code, password or other means of access, commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to both.

### Identity theft

22. (1) Any person who, intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose, by means of a computer system, obtains, supplies, uses or has in his possession or control, any identity information of another person with the intention to commit or facilitate the commission of any offence under any written law, commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), “identity information” means any information that identifies, or is capable of identifying or purports to identify, a person.

<!-- page 20 -->

### Transmitting, etc., content generated or manipulated by means

### of computer system

23. Any person who transmits, distributes, publishes, sells or offers for sale or otherwise makes available, any visual or audio content generated or manipulated, whether wholly or partly, by means of a computer system, resembling an existing person, object, place, entity or event that falsely appears to a person to be authentic or truthful with the intention to commit or facilitate the commission of any offence under any written law, whether by himself or by any other person, commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

### Dissemination of intimate images

24. (1) Any person who transmits, distributes, publishes, sells or offers for sale or otherwise makes available by means of a computer system any intimate images of any person, commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to both.

(2) Any person who commits an offence under subsection (1) with the intention to cause humiliation or harm to, or to coerce or intimidate, the person depicted in the intimate image, shall, on conviction, be liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to both.

(3) For the purposes of this section—

(a) “intimate image” means a visual recording, including a photograph or video recording, made by any means, whether altered or otherwise, that is sexual in nature, in which the sexual parts of a person are exposed or a person is engaged in sexual activity; and

(b) “harm” means harm to a person’s body, mind, reputation or property, including psychological harm.

<!-- page 21 -->

### Offences committed which affects or involves national critical

### information infrastructure entity or national critical information

### infrastructure

25. (1) Any person who commits an offence under Part III or IV or section 21, 22 or 23 which affects or involves a national critical information infrastructure entity or national critical information infrastructure, shall, on conviction—

(a) where such offence involves loss of life, be liable to imprisonment for a term of not less than thirty years but not exceeding forty years or to a fine not exceeding two million ringgit or to both;

(b) where such offence involves any injury to any person, be liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding fifteen years or to a fine not exceeding one million and five hundred thousand ringgit or to both; and

(c) in any other case, be liable to a fine not exceeding one million ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten years or to both.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), “national critical information infrastructure entity” and “national critical information infrastructure” have the meaning assigned to them in the Cyber Security Act 2024 [Act 854].

## Part VII

## ENFORCEMENT

### Authorization of public officer or officer of Commission

26. (1) The Minister may in writing authorize—

(a) any public officer; or

(b) with the concurrence of the Minister charged with the responsibility for communications, any officer of the Commission,

to exercise the powers of enforcement under this Act.

<!-- page 22 -->

(2) Any such officer authorized under subsection (1) shall be deemed to be a public servant within the meaning of the Penal Code.

(3) There shall be issued to each officer authorized under subsection (1) an authority card to be signed by the Minister.

(4) Whenever an officer authorized under subsection (1) exercises any of the powers under this Act, he shall, on demand, produce to the person against whom the power is being exercised the authority card issued to him under subsection (3).

### Power of investigation

27. (1) An authorized officer shall have all the powers necessary to carry out an investigation under this Act.

(2) An authorized officer shall have the powers of a police officer of whatever rank as provided for under the Criminal Procedure Code [Act 593] in relation to investigation into any offence under this Act, and such powers shall be in addition to and not in derogation of, the powers provided for under this Act.

### Search and seizure with warrant

28. (1) Where it appears to a Magistrate, upon written information on oath from an authorized officer and after such inquiry as the Magistrate considers necessary, that there is reasonable cause to believe that—

(a) any premises have been used in; or

(b) there is in any premises evidence necessary to the carrying out of an investigation into,

the commission of an offence under this Act, the Magistrate may issue a warrant authorizing the authorized officer named in the warrant, at any reasonable time by day or by night and with or without assistance, to enter the premises and if need be by force.

<!-- page 23 -->

(2) Without affecting the generality of subsection (1), the warrant issued by the Magistrate may authorize an authorized officer—

(a) to search and seize—

(i) any computer system or part of the computer system;

(ii) any computer data in a computer system or part of the computer system;

(iii) any computer data storage medium containing computer data;

(iv) any book, account, document, signboard, card, letter, pamphlet, leaflet or notice, which contains or are reasonably suspected to contain information as to any offence so suspected to have been committed; or

(v) any other document, facility, apparatus, vehicle, equipment, device, thing or matter that is reasonably believed to furnish evidence of the commission of the offence;

(b) to activate any on-site computer system and computer data storage medium;

(c) to make and retain a copy of computer data;

(d) to maintain the integrity of the relevant computer data;

(e) to render inaccessible or remove computer data in the accessed computer system; or

(f) to take a printout of output of computer data.

(3) An authorized officer conducting a search under subsection (1) may, for the purpose of investigating into the offence, search any person who is in or on the premises.

<!-- page 24 -->

(4) An authorized officer making a search of a person under subsection (3) may seize or take possession of, and place in safe custody all things, other than the necessary clothing, found upon the person, and any other things, for which there is a reason to believe that they are the instruments or other evidence of the crime, and they may be detained until the discharge or acquittal of the person.

(5) No person shall be searched except by another person of the same gender, and such search shall be conducted with strict regard to decency.

(6) If, by reason of its nature, size or amount, it is not practicable to remove any computer system or part of the computer system, computer data, computer data storage medium, book, account, document, signboard, card, letter, pamphlet, leaflet, notice, facility, apparatus, vehicle, equipment, device, thing or matter seized under this section, the authorized officer who effected the seizure shall, by any means, seal such computer system or part of the computer system, computer data, computer data storage medium, book, account, document, signboard, card, letter, pamphlet, leaflet, notice, facility, apparatus, vehicle, equipment, device, thing or matter in or on the premises in which it is found.

(7) Any person who, without lawful authority, breaks, tampers with or damages the seal referred to in subsection (6) or removes any computer system or part of the computer system, computer data, computer data storage medium, book, account, document, signboard, card, letter, pamphlet, leaflet, notice, facility, apparatus, vehicle, equipment, device, thing or matter under seal or attempts to do so commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years or to both.

(8) If a search under this section indicates that there is any interference-causing equipment, radio apparatus or radio-sensitive equipment causing interference to the search, the authorized officer may direct the owner or the person in control of the equipment or apparatus or the premises in which the equipment or apparatus is located to take necessary steps to eliminate the interference.

<!-- page 25 -->

### Search and seizure without warrant

29. If an authorized officer is satisfied upon information received that he has reasonable cause to believe that by reason of delay in obtaining a search warrant under section 28 the investigation would be adversely affected or evidence of the commission of an offence is likely to be tampered with, removed, damaged or destroyed, the authorized officer may enter the premises and exercise in, upon and in respect of the premises all the powers referred to in section 28 in as full and ample a manner as if he were authorized to do so by a warrant issued under that section.

### Warrant admissible notwithstanding defect

30. A search warrant issued under this Act shall be valid and enforceable notwithstanding any defect, mistake or omission in the search warrant or in the application for such warrant and any computer system or part of the computer system, computer data, computer data storage medium, book, account, document, signboard, card, letter, pamphlet, leaflet, notice, facility, apparatus, vehicle, equipment, device, thing or matter seized under such warrant shall be admissible in evidence in any proceedings under this Act.

### List of things seized

31. (1) Where any seizure is made under this Part, an authorized officer making the seizure shall prepare a list of computer system or part of the computer system, computer data, computer data storage medium, book, account, document, signboard, card, letter, pamphlet, leaflet, notice, facility, apparatus, vehicle, equipment, device, thing or matter seized and of the premises in which it is found and shall sign the list.

(2) The list prepared in accordance with subsection (1) shall be delivered immediately to—

(a) the occupant of the premises where any computer system or part of the computer system, computer data, computer data storage medium, book, account, document, signboard, card, letter, pamphlet, leaflet, notice, facility, apparatus, vehicle, equipment, device, thing or matter seized is found; and

<!-- page 26 -->

(b) the person in control or in charge of the vehicle, where the seizure is made under section 29.

(3) Where the seizure is made in or from any premises which are unoccupied, the authorized officer shall whenever possible post a list of things seized conspicuously at the premises.

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

This Bill (“the proposed Act”) seeks to repeal the Computer Crimes Act 1997 [Act 563] and to establish a new and comprehensive legal framework to prevent and combat cybercrimes, being offences provided for under Parts III to VI of the proposed Act and offences under any other written law committed by means of a computer system. The proposed Act seeks to strengthen the legal framework relating to cybercrimes through the criminalization of certain acts involving the use of computer systems and the strengthening of powers relating to the investigation and prosecution of cybercrimes. The proposed Act is necessitated by the rapid advancement of digital technology, the borderless nature of cyberspace and the increasing sophistication of cybercrime activities which threaten national security, the economy, public order and the safety of persons. In drafting the proposed Act, consideration has also been given to relevant international instruments relating to cybercrimes, including the Budapest Convention on Cybercrime and the United Nations Convention against Cybercrime, in line with Malaysia’s commitment towards strengthening efforts in addressing cybercrime-related threats. The proposed Act provides, among others, for the extra-territorial application of offences, the establishment of the Committee on Combating Cybercrimes and various categories of offences, including offences relating to the confidentiality, integrity and availability of computer system and computer data, offences relating to computer-related forgery and computer-related fraud, offences relating to the national digital identity service and other cybercrime-related offences. The proposed Act further provides for enforcement powers, the establishment of an integrated information system, the duties of service providers as well as other related provisions.

Clause 11 seeks to provide that any person who commits an offence under clause 10 of the proposed Act with the intention to commit a further offence involving fraud or dishonesty or which causes injury as defined in the Penal Code [Act 574] or to facilitate the commission of such further offence whether by himself or by any other person commits an offence under this clause and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

Clause 12 seeks to provide for the offence of intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose intercepting by technical means, any non-public transmission of computer data to, from or within a computer system including any electromagnetic emissions from a computer system carrying computer data. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

17. Clause 13 seeks to provide for the offence of intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose damaging, deleting, deteriorating, altering, suppressing or modifying computer data, rendering computer data meaningless, useless or ineffective, obstructing, interrupting or interfering with the lawful use of computer data or denying access to computer data to any person entitled to it. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years or to both.

18. Clause 14 seeks to provide for the offence of intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose inputting, transmitting, damaging, deleting, deteriorating, altering or suppressing computer data so as to seriously hinder or interfere with the functioning of a computer system or lawful use or operation of a computer system. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

19. Clause 15 seeks to provide for the offence of intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose obtaining, procuring for using, producing, selling, importing or exporting, distributing, disclosing or otherwise making available a device including a program, that is designed or adapted primarily for the purpose of committing any offence under the proposed Act or a password, access credentials, electronic signature or similar computer data by which the whole or any part of a computer system or computer data is capable of being accessed with the intention that it be used by himself or any other person for the purpose of committing an offence under the proposed Act. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both. This clause also seeks to provide that any person who has in his possession or control any of the device or password, access credentials, electronic signature or computer data referred to in

<!-- page 47 -->

subclause 15(1) of the proposed Act with the intention that the device or password, access credentials, electronic signature or computer data be used by himself or by any other person for the purpose of committing any offence under the proposed Act, commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to both. An example of a situation in which a person commits an offence under this clause is where the person produces a computer program with the intention that the program be used to create fake websites to obtain information from members of the public who access such websites, as the program is capable of being used by another person to commit computer-related fraud under clause 17. A person who procures for use such computer program with the intention of committing computer-related fraud also commits an offence under this clause.

## Part IV

20. Part IV of the proposed Act provides for offences relating to computer-related forgery and computer-related fraud.

21. Clause 16 seeks to provide for the offence of computer-related forgery, being the offence of intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose inputting, altering, deleting or suppressing computer data resulting in inauthentic computer data with the intention that such computer data be considered or acted upon for any legal purpose as if the computer data were authentic, whether or not such computer data is intended to deceive any particular person. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, in the case where valuable security is involved, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both or in any other case, be liable to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or both.

22. Clause 17 seeks to provide for the offence of computer-related fraud, being the offence of intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose inputting, altering, deleting or suppressing any computer data, interfering with the functioning of a computer system or causing deception as to factual circumstances by means of a computer system that causes a person to do or omit to do anything which that person would not otherwise do or omit to do, so as to cause loss of property to another person with the fraudulent or dishonest intention to procure without authority economic benefit for himself or for another person. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one million ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten years or to both.

<!-- page 48 -->

## Part V

23. Part V of the proposed Act provides for offences relating to national digital identity service, which is the electronic service owned by the Federal Government by which a user’s identity is verified and authenticated.

24. Clause 18 contains the definitions of “credential”, “user” and “national digital identity service” used in Part V of the proposed Act. The term “user” is defined as a person who is registered with the national digital identity service.

25. Clause 19 seeks to provide for the offence of a user disclosing his national digital identity service password or providing any other means of securing access in the identity of the user to any program or computer data in any computer system by means of the national digital identity service, to any person knowing or having reasonable grounds to believe that the purpose of the disclosure or provision is for any person to commit or to facilitate the commission by any person of, any offence under any written law. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years or to both. This clause also seeks to provide for a rebuttable presumption that the user has reasonable grounds to believe that the disclosure or provision is for the commission of, or to facilitate the commission of, an offence under any written law if it is proved in any proceedings for an offence under this clause that the user discloses his national digital identity service password or provides any other means of securing access in the identity of the user to any person for any gain whether or not the gain is a wrongful gain or realized or knowing that the disclosure or provision is likely to cause wrongful loss to any person.

26. Clause 20 seeks to provide for the offence of obtaining, retaining, supplying, offering to supply, transmitting or otherwise making available any credential of any user for the purpose of committing or facilitating the commission of, any offence under any written law or knowing or having reason to believe that the credential will be used, or is likely to be used, for the commission of, or to facilitate the commission of, any offence under any written law. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years or to both, and for a second or subsequent offence, to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to both.

## Part VI

27. Part VI of the proposed Act provides for other offences relating to cybercrimes such as wrongful communication, identity theft, transmission of content generated or manipulated by means of computer system and dissemination of intimate images. This Part also provides for enhanced punishments for certain offences under the proposed Act which affects or involves national critical information infrastructure entities or national critical information infrastructure.

<!-- page 49 -->

28. Clause 21 seeks to provide for the offence of communicating directly or indirectly a number, code, password or other means of access to a computer system to any person other than a person to whom he is duly authorized to communicate the number, code, password or other means of access to the computer system. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to both.

29. Clause 22 seeks to provide for the offence of identity theft being the offence of intentionally and without authority or without lawful purpose by means of a computer system, obtaining, supplying, using or having in possession or control any identity information of another person with the intention to commit or facilitate the commission of any offence under any written law. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

30. Clause 23 seeks to provide for the offence of transmitting, distributing, publishing, selling or offering for sale or otherwise making available, any visual or audio content generated or manipulated, whether wholly or partly, by means of a computer system, resembling existing persons, objects, places, entities or events that falsely appears to a person to be authentic or truthful with the intention to commit or facilitate the commission of any offence under any written law, whether by himself or by any other person. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding five hundred thousand ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding seven years or to both.

31. Clause 24 seeks to provide for the offence of disseminating intimate images of any person by transmitting, distributing, publishing, selling or offering for sale or otherwise making available the intimate image by means of a computer system. Any person who commits an offence under this clause shall, on conviction, be liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand ringgit or to both. This clause also seeks to provide for enhanced punishment where an offence under this clause is committed with the intention to cause humiliation or harm to, or to coerce or intimidate, the person depicted in the intimate image. Based on the definition of “intimate image” provided for under the proposed Act, the intimate image may include images, videos or visual depictions that are wholly or partly generated, manipulated, altered or synthesized through artificial intelligence, machine learning, deepfake technology or other automated digital technologies, including content generated using generative artificial intelligence platforms and such image depicts a person’s sexual parts or portrays the person as being engaged in sexual activity. This clause is intended to address the misuse of emerging technologies to create and disseminate fabricated or manipulated intimate images, irrespective of whether the image is authentic, digitally altered or entirely computer-generated.

<!-- page 50 -->

32. Clause 25 seeks to provide for enhanced punishment for commission of offences under Parts III and IV and clauses 21, 22 and 23 of the proposed Act which affect or involve a national critical information infrastructure entity or national critical information infrastructure. Any person who commits such offence shall, on conviction, where the offence causes loss of life, be liable to imprisonment for a term of not less than thirty years but not exceeding forty years or to a fine not exceeding two million ringgit or to both. Where such offence causes any injury to any person, be liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding fifteen years or to a fine not exceeding one million and five hundred thousand ringgit or to both and in any other case be liable to a fine not exceeding one million ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten years or to both.

## Part VII

33. Part VII of the proposed Act deals with enforcement provisions.

34. Clause 26 seeks to provide that the Minister may authorize any public officer, or with the concurrence of the Minister charged with the responsibility for communications, any officer of the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission, to exercise the powers of enforcement under the proposed Act.

35. Clause 27 seeks to provide for the power of investigation of the authorized officers.

36. Clause 28 deals with the provision relating to search and seizure with warrant.

37. Clause 29 deals with the provision relating to search and seizure without warrant.

38. Clause 30 seeks to provide that a search warrant issued under the proposed Act shall be valid and enforceable notwithstanding any defect, mistake or omission in the search warrant.

39. Clause 31 seeks to require an authorized officer to prepare a list of things seized in the course of his investigation.

40. Clause 32 seeks to provide that the cost of holding any thing seized under the proposed Act pending the completion of criminal proceedings against a person for an offence under the proposed Act shall be a debt due to the Federal Government by such person and shall be recoverable upon conviction of such person.

41. Clause 33 seeks to provide for the release of any thing seized under the proposed Act.

42. Clause 34 deals with the provision relating to the forfeiture of any thing seized under the proposed Act.

<!-- page 51 -->

43. Clause 35 seeks to provide that any thing forfeited or deemed to be forfeited under the proposed Act shall be the property of the Federal Government.

44. Clause 36 seeks to provide for the access to computer system and computer data by the authorized officer.

45. Clause 37 seeks to provide that no cost or damages arising from the seizure of any thing under the proposed Act to be recoverable in any proceedings before any court unless such seizure was made without reasonable cause.

46. Clause 38 seeks to provide that if an authorized officer is satisfied that any computer data is reasonably required for the purposes of an investigation into an offence under the proposed Act and there is a risk that the computer data may be destroyed or rendered inaccessible, the authorized officer may, by written notice given to a person in possession or control of the computer system where the computer data is contained, require the person to ensure that the computer data be preserved for the period and in the manner as may be specified in the written notice.

47. Clause 39 seeks to empower an authorized officer to require by written notice a person in possession or control of the computer system where the computer data is contained to disclose such computer data in the manner as specified in the written notice if the authorized officer is satisfied that the computer data is reasonably required for the purposes of an investigation into an offence under the proposed Act.

48. Clause 40 seeks to provide that a Public Prosecutor may, on the application of an authorized officer, require any service provider to collect or record traffic data in real-time by technical means and provide the traffic data to the authorized officer within the service provider’s existing technical capability, if the Public Prosecutor considers that the traffic data associated with specified communications transmitted by means of a computer system is relevant for the purposes of any investigation into an offence under the proposed Act.

49. Clause 41 seeks to provide that a Public Prosecutor may, on the application of an authorized officer, require the service provider, within the service provider’s technical capability, to intercept or retain a specified communication or communications of a specified description, to collect or record, by technical means any content data, in real-time, contained in such communications or to assist the authorized officer in the interception, retention, collection or recording if the Public Prosecutor considers that the communications is likely to contain content data which is relevant for the purposes of an investigation into an offence under the proposed Act.

50. Clause 42 seeks to provide that an authorized officer may require the attendance of any person acquainted with the facts and circumstances of a case being investigated by the authorized officer.

51. Clause 43 seeks to provide that an authorized officer may examine any person supposed to be acquainted with the facts and circumstances of a case being investigated by the authorized officer.

<!-- page 52 -->

52. Clause 44 seeks to provide for the admissibility of a statement made by a person to an authorized officer in the course of an investigation made under the proposed Act as evidence.

53. Clause 45 deals with additional powers of an authorized officer for the purposes of the execution of the proposed Act.

54. Clause 46 seeks to provide that it shall be an offence for any person who obstructs any authorized officer in the performance of their duties under the proposed Act.

55. Clause 47 seeks to provide that for the purposes of search and seizure without warrant under clause 29, access to computer system or computer data under clause 36 and interception of content data under clause 41, where the authorized officer is a police officer, the powers under those clauses shall only be exercised by a police officer not below the rank of Inspector.

56. Clause 48 seeks to provide that, notwithstanding any other written law, clauses 36, 38, 39, 40 and 41 of the proposed Act shall also apply in respect of any investigation into any offences committed by means of a computer system under any other written law and the collection of evidence in electronic form for the purposes of any investigation or proceedings relating to an offence under any written law.

57. Clause 49 seeks to provide for the jurisdiction of a Sessions Court to try any offence under the proposed Act notwithstanding any written law to the contrary.

## Part VIII

58. Part VIII of the proposed Act deals with miscellaneous provisions.

59. Clause 50 seeks to provide for the establishment, administration, maintenance and management of an integrated information system by the Chief Executive of the National Cyber Security Agency (“Chief Executive”) which contains information received or gathered by the Chief Executive which may be used to prevent and investigate cybercrimes.

60. Clause 51 seeks to provide for the general duty of service provider to take necessary measures to prevent any services that it provides from being used for the commission of cybercrimes. A service provider who fails to comply with the written notice given under this clause commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one million ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten years or to both.

<!-- page 53 -->

61. Clause 52 seeks to empower the Minister, where the Minister is satisfied that it is necessary in the interest of national security or public safety and proportionate to the purpose of the prevention, investigation, detection or prosecution of any offence under any written law, to require a service provider, by an order published in the Gazette, to retain certain computer data other than content data. This clause further seeks to provide for the particulars to be specified in the order, including the type or category of computer data which shall be retained, the retention period of the computer data, the method of use of the computer data and any other particulars which the Minister deems necessary for the purposes of the retention of the computer data. This clause also seeks to provide that a service provider who fails to comply with the order commits an offence and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding one million ringgit or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding ten years or to both.

62. Clause 53 seeks to provide that any service provider or any of his employees, shall not be liable in any criminal or civil proceedings of any nature, for any damages, including punitive damages, loss, cost or expenditure suffered or to be suffered, whether directly or indirectly, for any act or omission done in good faith, in the performance of the duty imposed under clause 51 of the proposed Act.

63. Clause 54 seeks to empower the court to make certain orders upon application by the Public Prosecutor or authorized officer if it appears to the court that there is reasonable likelihood that any person will contravene any provision of the proposed Act or any person has contravened a provision of the proposed Act and that there are steps which could be taken to remedy the contravention.

64. Clause 55 seeks to provide for the service of notice or other documents required to be served on any person under the proposed Act.

65. Clause 56 seeks to provide that any person who attempts to commit or abets the commission of any offence under the proposed Act shall, on conviction, be liable to the punishment provided for such offence.

66. Clause 57 seeks to provide for the protection of the Minister, any member of the Committee, the Chief Executive or any authorized officer against suits and legal proceedings in respect of any act, neglect or default done or omitted by him in the course of carrying out his duties under the proposed Act.

67. Clause 58 seeks to provide that no prosecution shall be instituted for any offence under the proposed Act without the written consent of the Public Prosecutor.

68. Clause 59 deals with the liability of a director, compliance officer, partner, manager, secretary or other similar officer in respect of an offence by a company, limited liability partnership, firm, society or other body of persons under the proposed Act.

<!-- page 54 -->

69. Clause 60 deals with the liability of a person for any act, omission, neglect or default by an employee of the person, agent of the person or employee of the person’s agent under the proposed Act.

70. Clause 61 seeks to provide for the repeal and savings provisions.
